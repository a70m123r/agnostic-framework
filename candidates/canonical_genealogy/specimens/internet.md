### The surprise (synergy) — **conf 0.85** (decoupled from child 0.92, R9)

The genuine joint-surprise is **NOT that a data network exists** — single packet networks (ARPANET, NPL, CYCLADES, the X.25 publics) were implied by packet-switching alone. It is **INTERNETWORKING**: that independently-owned, heterogeneous, mutually-distrusting networks could federate into one seamless whole with **no central control, no common owner, and no internal change** to any member — and that this open, end-to-end, "dumb-network/smart-edges" architecture would scale to a planet and **outcompete every centrally-planned alternative.**

- **Packet-switching alone** yields *silos* (each network a walled garden).
- **The open-architecture principle alone** is an empty stance with nothing to connect.
- **Standout sub-surprise:** the winning architecture was the **less-controlled** one — the camp with no central authority, no government mandate, "rough consensus and running code," beat the camp (OSI/X.25) backed by ISO, the CCITT, the PTTs and government procurement mandates (GOSIP).
- **The phantom (cf. Manhattan's phantom enemy):** the heavily-funded, officially-mandated OSI stack was believed **inevitable** ("OSI is now inevitable" — Metcalfe 1989) and **never shipped at scale.**

**`surprise_priority` (R15):** the internetworking surprise pre-existed the weld as a **conjecture-in-a-parent** — Licklider's "Intergalactic Computer Network" memos (1962-63) imagined a globe-spanning interconnected network ~11 years before the 1974 specification (analogous to Faraday's 1846 light-guess and Szilard's 1933 chain-reaction patent). conf 0.85.
