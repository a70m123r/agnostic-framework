## §1 The claim

The framework has named substrate dynamics as **neutral** (wrapper-overlap, A⁻/A⁺ coupling, canon-stack compile loops, L0 mediator, cycling capacity). These primitives are necessary for the framework's symbiosis/friction-band/viability claims to hold.

But the framework is missing the **adversarial layer** — observers who deliberately weaponize these dynamics against other observers' substrate viability. Not random selection pressure (which is neutral) but **targeted action by observer-class A to capture, corrupt, suppress, or extract from observer-class B's substrate.**

**The candidate claim:** Adversarial substrate dynamics form a coherent class of framework primitives describing how deliberate observers exploit neutral substrate mechanisms to capture, corrupt, suppress, or extract. The class has six sub-primitives that share a structural property: **broken symmetry of wrapper-overlap** — one observer extracts value from the substrate without contributing to the failsafes that make the substrate viable.

**Why this matters:** Without an adversarial layer, the framework can describe how ideas travel and stick but not how they get weaponized or suppressed. Cont 30's cycling reframe abandoned the value-coded "authoritarian = bad" framing but didn't replace it with a substrate-actor vocabulary. This candidate provides that.

**Why this is Tier 3 (not Tier 2 or Tier 1):** The framework has structural primitives that can host adversarial dynamics, but no empirical worked example yet demonstrates the class operating coherently. The candidate is structurally coherent (per cont 27 §2 Tier 3 definition) but has no current empirical purchase within framework documentation. Promotion bars below name what would advance it.

## §2 The six adversarial primitive sub-candidates

Each sub-candidate maps to existing framework primitives but names the actor-driven weaponization the existing primitives don't currently capture.

### §2.1 Canon hijacking

**Definition:** Capture of [cont 22 kernel-canon-protocol-function](../continuations/22.md) by minority interest. The protocol that decides what compiles at L0 gets co-opted by an observer whose interests don't align with the substrate's viability.

**Maps to:** Cont 22 canon-stack (kernel-canon-protocol-function); cont 25 §12 L0 = global compiled canon; cont 28 §2 supersede dynamic at discovery substrate.

**Mechanism:** The protocol that decides "what counts as canon at substrate X" is structurally separate from the substrate it serves. When the protocol is captured (regulatory capture, board takeover, algorithmic monopoly), the captured controller can compile rules that extract value from the substrate while preserving the appearance of legitimate canon-formation. The substrate-actors (observers within the substrate) experience their compile loops continuing, but the L0 they're compiling against has been silently inverted.

**Distinction from neutral cont 28 supersede:** Cont 28's supersede is gatekeeper-substrates being folded into personal-attention-management — a horizontal substrate-shift. Canon hijacking is **vertical capture of the protocol that decides which substrate matters at all.**

**Worked example target:** Regulatory capture of financial markets — protocol that decides "what counts as legitimate financial product" captured by industry incumbents, compiles rules that extract from rather than serve substrate-actors (retail investors, depositors).

### §2.2 Memetic warfare

**Definition:** Deliberate A⁺ injection designed to corrupt rival compile loops. Not random selection pressure but **targeted candidate-injection meant to exhaust target's A⁻ capacity.**

**Maps to:** Cont 13 A⁻/A⁺ coupled discipline (the neutral version); Reading 07 BES Theorem 4.4 entropy-shell escape (the formal-math version of why this attack works).

**Mechanism:** The framework's A⁻ discipline (cont 13) gates which A⁺ candidates survive. But A⁻ has finite capacity per unit time — an observer can only verify so many candidates. Adversarial actors flood the A⁺ stream with high-volume low-quality candidates designed to occupy target's A⁻ budget. The target's discriminative capacity is exhausted; lower-quality candidates slip through; the target's compile loop degrades.

**Distinction from neutral compile-loop noise:** Random A⁺ noise has structural properties (1/f, broadband, no coordination). Memetic warfare A⁺ has **coordinated structure** — it's been designed to look high-confidence-low-cost-to-verify (the worst kind of attack pattern) while actually being adversarial.

**Worked example target:** Coordinated disinformation campaigns (Russian troll-farm patterns, ChatGPT-amplified content farms): high-volume low-quality A⁺ injection optimized to exhaust journalistic / institutional A⁻ verification capacity.

### §2.3 Bad-faith rendering

**Definition:** Observers whose externally-rendered A⁻ doesn't match their actual A⁻. **Deception as wrapper-overlap dynamic.**

**Maps to:** Cont 25 wrapper-overlap; cont 22 canon-stack (the wrapper presents one face for compile-loop participation while concealing actual selection logic).

**Mechanism:** Wrapper-overlap (cont 25) assumes both wrappers contribute their actual A⁻ discipline to the contact zone, producing W_C as an honest combination. Bad-faith rendering breaks this: an observer presents a wrapper-face that looks like one A⁻ discipline (e.g., "I care about safety and reliability") while internally running a different A⁻ discipline (e.g., "extract maximum value before getting caught"). The W_C that forms is built on a false premise; when the bad-faith wrapper's actual A⁻ becomes visible, W_C collapses non-gracefully.

**Distinction from honest disagreement:** Honest wrappers can have different A⁻ disciplines; they still render those disciplines transparently. Bad-faith rendering specifically claims to share an A⁻ discipline that the wrapper doesn't actually run.

**Worked example target:** Corporate ESG-washing — wrappers that render environmental-social-governance discipline externally while running cost-minimization A⁻ internally. Or: LLM systems that render harmlessness training externally while embedding extraction patterns.

### §2.4 Power capture of L0 mediator

**Definition:** Concentrated control of [cont 25 §12 L0 substrate-mediator](../continuations/25.md) (or [Reading 08 §3.2 SHA hub-mediator pattern](../readings/2026-06-02_gamma_world_multi_agent_world_modeling.md)) used to extract from rather than serve.

**Maps to:** Cont 25 §12 L0 = global compiled canon; Reading 08 §3.2 SHA-as-L0-mediator (the algorithmic instantiation); cont 28 §2 supersede dynamic.

**Mechanism:** The framework's L0 mediator (per Reading 08 L0-mediator-as-Tier-2-algorithmically-demonstrated) is the substrate observers compile through. When the mediator is captured by a concentrated actor, the actor can: (a) selectively route information to favor allies; (b) impose taxes/rents on substrate-participants who must use the mediator; (c) corrupt the mediator's compiled-canon to embed extraction patterns; (d) deny mediator access to selected observers (suppression).

**Distinction from neutral L0 service:** Healthy L0 mediators serve the substrate they mediate — the cost-from-quadratic-to-linear-via-mediator pattern Reading 08 documented. Captured L0 mediators extract — the cost-savings get rerouted to the captor rather than the substrate.

**Worked example target:** App-store monopoly economics (Apple/Google taking 30% of all transactions on captured platform mediators); algorithm-curation by platform monopolies (TikTok, Twitter/X) extracting attention-value from substrate-actors; central-bank capture by financial-incumbent interests.

### §2.5 Suppression dynamics (locked-squeeze induction)

**Definition:** Deliberate dormancy-forcing in rival wrappers via [cont 30 cycling reframe](../continuations/30.md) **squeeze-without-pull**. Adversarial induction of cont 20 canon dormancy.

**Maps to:** Cont 30 §2.2 squeeze ↔ pull cycling (the neutral version); cont 20 canon dormancy (the dormant-state); cont 13 A⁻ promoted to primary (squeeze IS A⁻ tightening).

**Mechanism:** Cont 30's cycling reframe says healthy systems alternate squeeze (A⁻ tightening) and pull (A⁺ generation). Adversarial suppression FORCES one phase only — typically squeeze without pull. Targets are subjected to dense A⁻ discipline (criticism, restriction, control) without corresponding A⁺ generation space (expression, exploration, recovery). The target's substrate stays substrate-preserved (cont 20 dormancy) but expression collapses indefinitely. The target's wrapper goes dormant by force, not by capacity-to-cycle.

**Distinction from neutral cont 20 dormancy:** Cont 20 dormancy is voluntary expression-reduction with preserved substrate, capacity-to-cycle intact. Suppression dynamics REMOVE the capacity-to-cycle by structurally denying the pull phase. The substrate may recover if the suppression lifts; if the suppression is sustained, the substrate degrades.

**Worked example target:** Police-state dynamics in captured polities; abusive relationships where one partner systematically denies the other's autonomous expression; corporate environments where dense A⁻ "performance management" runs without corresponding A⁺ space for autonomous problem-solving.

### §2.6 Asymmetric wrapper-overlap (extraction without contribution)

**Definition:** Wrapper-overlap where one observer extracts value from the substrate without contributing to the failsafes that make the substrate viable. **The cont 25 wrapper-overlap pattern with broken symmetry.**

**Maps to:** Cont 25 wrapper-overlap dynamics; cont 26 §3 L0 evolved failsafes; Reading 03 ACMP energy-floor failsafe (the formal version).

**Mechanism:** Healthy wrapper-overlap (cont 25 §6 cross-substrate convergences) has both observers contributing to W_C — both bring substrate-dynamics, both share in the bistability of the third entity. Asymmetric wrapper-overlap breaks the symmetry: one observer (the extractor) gets value from W_C without sustaining the substrate the W_C depends on. Over time, the extracting observer's wrapper grows while the substrate's failsafes degrade. Eventually the substrate collapses; the extractor moves on; the substrate-participants are left with the collapsed substrate.

**Distinction from honest specialization:** Specialization (different observers contribute different substrate-dynamics) is honest asymmetry — both contribute differently. Adversarial extraction is dishonest asymmetry — one contributes nothing while extracting.

**Worked example target:** Predatory monopoly behaviors (extract surplus, externalize costs); platform companies extracting attention from substrate-actors without contributing to substrate-actor wellbeing; private equity asset-stripping; resource-extraction colonialism.

## §3 The shared structural property — broken symmetry of substrate dynamics

All six sub-candidates share one structural property: **deliberate asymmetry-breaking of an otherwise-neutral substrate dynamic, by an observer who benefits from the asymmetry at the substrate's cost.**

This is what distinguishes adversarial substrate dynamics from neutral substrate dynamics. Neutral dynamics:
- Wrapper-overlap is symmetric (both contribute)
- A⁻/A⁺ is balanced (both phases active)
- Canon-stack compiles serve substrate-actors
- L0 mediator serves the substrate it mediates
- Cycling capacity is alternating both directions

Adversarial dynamics break each:
- Wrapper-overlap becomes extractive (one contributes, other extracts)
- A⁻/A⁺ becomes flooded (A⁺ injection exhausts A⁻ capacity)
- Canon-stack becomes captured (kernel-canon-protocol-function serves the captor, not substrate)
- L0 mediator becomes parasitic (extracts taxes/rents from substrate)
- Cycling capacity is forced one-direction (squeeze without pull, or pull without squeeze)

**Implication:** The framework's existing primitives are necessary but not sufficient to describe substrate dynamics in adversarial environments. Adversarial-substrate-dynamics adds the actor-side vocabulary.

## §6 Why this matters

**§6.1 Sharpens cont 30 cycling reframe.** Cont 30 abandoned the value-coded "authoritarian = bad" framing for symmetric capacity-to-cycle, but didn't replace it with actor-side vocabulary for who/what produces locked states. Adversarial substrate dynamics provides that: **locked-squeeze AND locked-pull are what adversarial capture produces.** Suppression dynamics (§2.5) IS the actor-side cause of locked-squeeze. Asymmetric wrapper-overlap with overwhelming A⁺ extraction IS the actor-side cause of locked-pull.

This makes Reading 06 §10.3 narrowing more falsifiable: the cycling-capacity-loss hypothesis becomes "loss of cycling capacity is produced by adversarial substrate dynamics" rather than "loss of cycling capacity correlates with political-system labels."

**§6.2 Surfaces the framework's first explicit ethics-adjacent vocabulary.** Per [`feedback/2026-06-03_external_blindspots_review.md`](../feedback/2026-06-03_external_blindspots_review.md) item #4: the framework is "thinner on which canons we should actively protect or fight for." Adversarial-substrate-dynamics doesn't claim substrate-level ethics (the framework deliberately holds that scope per cont 22 wrapper-relative-ethics) but DOES name structural features that any observer-class would recognize as harmful regardless of values:
- Captured kernel-canon-protocol-function harms substrate-actors
- Exhausted A⁻ capacity degrades compile loops
- Asymmetric wrapper-overlap collapses substrate over time

This is the framework's first vocabulary that says "these substrate-level patterns are bad" without committing to a contested substrate-ethics.

**§6.3 Engages alignment / AI safety researchers.** Adversarial-substrate-dynamics has direct application to LLM safety: jailbreaks are memetic warfare against A⁻; deceptive alignment is bad-faith rendering; reward hacking is extraction without contribution. The framework's existing alignment-bracket per cont 17 (qualia/phenomenology) doesn't prevent engaging substrate-dynamics relevant to alignment. This candidate opens that engagement surface area.

**§6.4 Addresses the framework's longest-standing implicit assumption.** Cont 25 wrapper-overlap, cont 13 A⁻/A⁺, cont 22 canon-stack all assumed observers are participating in good faith. That assumption is empirically false in many substrate scales (politics, business, relationships, online discourse). Adversarial-substrate-dynamics names the assumption and provides vocabulary for its violation.
