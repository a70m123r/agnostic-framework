### §2.3 Item 3 — Brackets subjective experience / qualia / phenomenology (Bucket B — deliberate scope)

**Framework response:** **Acknowledged deliberate scope limit per cont 17. The critic is correct that this limits applicability to consciousness / alignment work.**

[Cont 17](../continuations/17.md) named this scope decision explicitly: the framework "brackets consciousness questions agnostically" because the framework's claim is about structural observer-dynamics, not about phenomenal-experience-of-being-an-observer.

The honest response:
- The framework is not claiming completeness.
- The framework is not denying that qualia exist.
- The framework deliberately doesn't take a stance on hard-problem-of-consciousness because (a) the framework's claims hold whether qualia exist or not; (b) extending to qualia would require committing to a contested metaphysics that the framework's existing canon doesn't depend on.
- **For human-AI alignment work or consciousness questions specifically**, the framework would need extension. The critic is right.

**This is a scope-defense, not a gap-fix.** The framework could plausibly be extended to talk about phenomenal experience as a Tier 3 candidate later, but it would require either (a) external consciousness science maturing enough to provide non-contested Tier 1 anchors, OR (b) the framework's existing primitives being extended via worked examples in domains where qualia matter (e.g., LLM-self-report patterns).

**No action this round.** The bracket holds.

### §2.4 Item 4 — Descriptive power >> normative guidance (Bucket B — deliberate scope, with acknowledgment)

**Framework response:** **Genuine but structurally consistent with the framework's existing wrapper-relative-ethics claim. Could be MORE explicit.**

The framework's meta-discipline IS its normative commitment:

- Audit cadence — claims rigorous self-criticism is virtuous
- Cont 13 A⁻ promoted to primary — claims dense-feedback discipline is virtuous
- Cont 30 cycling reframe — claims capacity-to-cycle is healthier than locked-states

But the framework deliberately avoids **substrate-level normative claims** because — per the framework's own canon — normative ethics compile differently per observer-class. Cont 22 canon-stack vocabulary names this: top-canon (institutional norms) varies per observer-class; centre-canon (lived practice) varies per situation; sub-canon (intuitive default) varies per ring.

So the framework's lack of substrate-level normative guidance is itself a **framework claim**: normative ethics are wrapper-relative.

The critic is right that this looks like a gap. The framework's honest response is that the gap is a deliberate scope decision rooted in an existing structural commitment, but **the framework could be more explicit** about saying "our lack of substrate-ethics is itself a framework claim, not an evasion."

**Possible follow-up (queued, not actioned this round):** A future continuation or reading explicitly stating the wrapper-relative-ethics claim and naming what kinds of ethics-discussion the framework CAN engage (process-ethics, meta-ethics about how ethics-canon forms) vs CANNOT engage (substrate-ethics that claim to be universally true).
