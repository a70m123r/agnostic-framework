## §4. Structural integrity — does the new material stay consistent with the old?

Cross-cutting check: do the new primitives break or duplicate older ones? Three findings.

### 4.1 The A⁺/A⁻ trajectory is the cleanest evolution in the corpus

The same primitive (A⁺/A⁻) was refactored three times: introduced as operators in continuation 09; reframed as latent dimensions with charge-product algebra in continuation 13; rebalanced to asymmetric (A⁻ as primary) in continuation 15. Each refactor preserves the prior framing while adding structure. The continuation-13 charge-algebra framing still holds inside the continuation-15 asymmetry framing — they describe different aspects of the same primitive (interaction structure vs canon-formation priority). This is what coherent refactor looks like.

### 4.2 Potential duplication: viability band, criticality Γ, Wolfram four-class

Three primitives now do similar work from different angles:
- **Viability band** (cont 04): ρ_min < ρ(G) < ρ_max — substrate stays viable between sparseness and saturation.
- **Criticality Γ** (cont 04): Γ = rate(A⁺) / (rate(A⁻) + rate(stabilize)). Viability band sits at Γ ≈ 1.
- **Wolfram four-class** (cont 13): Class 4 (complex/organized) = the viability band as empirically observed across thousands of CAs.

Status: not duplication — the three are at different layers (graph topology / rate dynamics / observed behavior). But the framework would benefit from a single canon entry naming the relationship explicitly. Continuation 13 §2 makes the connection but doesn't formalize it. Recommendation: a future pass should add a meta-primitive ("substrate viability has three measurement faces") or merge the three entries with cross-references.

### 4.3 Potential conflict: A⁻ as "pruning" in cont 04 vs A⁻ as "primary compilation discipline" in cont 15

The conflict is real but resolved within continuation 15: the cont-15 refactor explicitly re-balances by appending the asymmetry note to the existing A⁺/A⁻ canon entry rather than replacing it. The "pruning" framing is preserved as the operator-level description; "primary discipline" is the new asymmetry-level description. This is the right kind of refactor — the old reading is still true at the operator level; the new reading is true at the architectural-priority level.

**Housekeeping debt:** the earlier continuations (09, 13) refer to A⁺/A⁻ in symmetric framing without acknowledging the cont-15 asymmetry. A future audit refresh should add a one-line note to each ("see cont 15 for the asymmetry refactor"). Continuation 15 §10 already flags this as housekeeping debt; v03 confirms it.

### 4.4 Compile-capacity enrichment (cont 12) vs multi-architecture universal approximation (cont 14)

Both deal with how different paths can reach the same compiled state. The continuation-12 framing is about the spiral substrate cycle returning with new restructuring sense (not new fuzzy). The continuation-14 framing is about multiple architectures converging on the same function at the limit.

Status: these are not the same primitive but they are adjacent. Compile-capacity enrichment is about the substrate gaining new compression mechanisms after a cycle; multi-architecture universal approximation is about different generator architectures reaching the same target at the limit. The connection is real — compile-capacity enrichment is what makes multi-architecture equivalence operationally relevant for finite-budget observers. A future continuation should make the connection explicit.

### 4.5 Imagine Engine (cont 12) vs A⁺ (cont 09 / 13 / 15)

The Imagine Engine is named in continuation 12 as the internal process generating phantom fuzzy. A⁺ is the operator/dimension that admits structure into canon. These are not the same primitive but they're at adjacent layers — A⁺ acts on what the Imagine Engine produces.

This is actually clear in the framework but the manifestos sometimes conflate them. Manifesto v4's "easy half" framing refers to A⁺ as the speculation-generation half of the loop; the Imagine Engine isn't named there. Either v5 should bring the Imagine Engine into the casual edition vocabulary, or the framework should explicitly fold the Imagine Engine into A⁺-as-generation-mechanism. Pick one; don't leave both floating.

### 4.6 Verdict on structural integrity

The framework maintains internal consistency across eleven continuations, which is a real achievement. The three points above are housekeeping, not foundational. The two cleanest examples of disciplined refactor are: (a) the A⁺/A⁻ trajectory through three rounds, and (b) the cone-profile primitive getting a "time as parameter, not substrate" inline note in cont 14 without disturbing its prior content. Both demonstrate that the framework's canon-update discipline is being applied to its own canon.

## §5. The asymmetry refactor — biggest structural shift

Continuation 15's A⁻-as-primary refactor is structurally the biggest change since v02. It deserves its own section in this audit because it ripples through earlier material.

**The shift:** A⁺/A⁻ had been treated as symmetric — both load-bearing, both equally part of canon-formation. Continuation 15 re-balances: A⁻ is the primary compilation discipline. A⁺ generates candidates (fluent, cheap, abundant — this is what most current AI does). A⁻ checks candidates against substrate constraints (scarce, disciplined, hard). What survives the check is canon. The pair is necessary together but the work is in the check.

**Why this matters operationally:**
- The framework's architectural prescription for agent systems sharpens: every fuzzy-generation step needs an explicit canon-compile pass. The framework now has a one-sentence claim about what's missing from current agent infrastructure: the verification layer.
- The framework's discipline of "viability requires both operators active" (cont 09 §6 etc.) reads differently — both *are* required, but their roles are not interchangeable.
- The "stale moat" failure mode (cont 04 §11) is now better-named: stale moats happen when an observer's A⁻ stops admitting new candidates (over-pruning, kernel-defense becomes blanket-rejection). The framework had this implicitly; the asymmetry framing makes it explicit.

**External evidence supporting the refactor:**
The four-carrier convergence (Aleph, MAMMAL, ANDI, Lippmann) in May 2026 is the strongest empirical claim the framework has made. Four substantively different systems landing in two weeks, all instantiating *checking-as-primary*, is unusual. Honest framing in cont 15 §1 acknowledges the small N and the selection-effect risk. v03 concurs: this is suggestive evidence, not conclusive — but the *shape* the framework predicted was on the record before the four carriers landed (since at least cont 09 implicitly, cont 13 explicitly via the charge algebra). Predicted-then-observed is a Lakatosian positive heuristic, even at N=4.

**What this refactor does to earlier material:**
- Continuation 04 §11 (scale-free graph compiler with two-operator agnostic): consistent with the refactor; A⁻ was already named as a peer operation. The new reading is that A⁻ is the load-bearing peer.
- Continuation 09 §5 (internal canon as observer-class with speculation as its scout): the speculation-as-scout framing is A⁺; the canon-as-observer's discipline is A⁻. The continuation is consistent but should be re-read with the asymmetry in mind.
- Continuation 12 §2 (Imagine Engine + bidirectional simulation): the Imagine Engine is on the generation side (A⁺); bidirectional simulation's detail-down direction is A⁻-dominant, meaning-up is A⁺-dominant. The two-direction framing maps cleanly onto the asymmetry — and the asymmetry framing predicts that the detail-down direction does more of the framework's actual epistemic work.
- Continuation 13's charge-product algebra: A⁺×A⁺ = constructive reinforcement, A⁻×A⁻ = compound pruning, A⁺×A⁻ = dialectical tension. The algebra survives the asymmetry; the cells of the Cayley table now have asymmetric weights but the structure is unchanged.

**What's now under-specified:**
- *What an A⁻ pass actually looks like* across different domains. Aleph uses Lean for formal reasoning; what does an A⁻ pass look like for conversational AI? For perception? For value learning? The framework now claims A⁻ is the primary discipline but has not yet specified what it operationally consists of outside formal-reasoning contexts.
- *How A⁻ avoids becoming a stale moat.* If A⁻ is the primary discipline, the failure mode of over-strict A⁻ (blanket-rejection of novel candidates) becomes the most important failure mode to avoid. Cont 15 §11 lists "constraint-checking vs creativity" as open question #3; v03 escalates this from open question to the framework's most urgent operational specification gap.

## §7. Weakest / highest-risk parts of 05–15

1. **The four-carrier convergence is N=4.** Cont 15 acknowledges this. The shape might be a selection effect; the framework should pre-register a prediction about what the next 90 days should show if the convergence is real (cont 15 open question #7 partially does this). Until the prediction lands or fails, the convergence is suggestive not conclusive.

2. **The Imagine Engine vs A⁺ ambiguity.** Two adjacent primitives without crisp relationship. Pick one as canonical; demote the other to "alternate name" or "operator-level vs process-level view."

3. **Compile-capacity enrichment and multi-architecture universal approximation should be explicitly connected.** Both are about path-multiplicity at the limit; the framework treats them separately.

4. **The substrate-cycle cluster (cont 12) introduced five new primitives in one round.** Cross-scale mediation, spiral substrate cycle, compile-capacity enrichment, bidirectional simulation, 0/not-0 foundation — each is plausible, but the load-test "do they survive contact with worked examples" hasn't been run on most of them. Cont 12 itself notes they should be operationalized over time. v03: prioritize.

5. **The monopole question / observer-dependence-of-distinction (cont 14 §6) is at unfalsifiability risk.** The candidate is interesting but recursively about its own answerability. Cont 14 §6's "escape hatch" — an observer-naive observation of phenotype-stable categorical asymmetry — is the right specification; the question is whether such an observation can be constructed. Until it can, the candidate sits at speculative-adjacent.

6. **The framework's primitive count is now ~50+.** Each individual primitive is defensible; the aggregate may be over-specified for a framework that has not yet shipped a working multi-agent implementation. Cont 10's "pick one and commit" discipline applies recursively: not every primitive earns its place equally, and a future audit pass should consider promoting the load-bearing ones and demoting the decorative ones.

7. **No quantitative test has been run on any framework prediction.** SDPO+ (cont 07), protein-model-on-packets (cont 10) — both proposed, neither executed. The framework is now operationally legible but still empirically untested in the sense of running its own predictions.

8. **The "live artifact" is now real, which exposes new risks.** The website existing means external readers can land on it and form impressions. The risk is no longer "no one can find the framework" — it's "people find it and bounce because the entry point is wrong." Manifesto v4 is the current best entry point but is not linked from the site (yet — pending the link-fix push). v03 escalates: hosting + first impression is now load-bearing.
