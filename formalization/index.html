The canon-stack equation

Observer as 4-tuple. Wrapper as stable union emerging from sub canon. Global canon as wrapper-render. Inferred kernel as subset of global canon. Recursion across rings from cell to human to earth. Information flow, bandwidth, and named canon protocols at each ring.

§01The sketch

What we are formalizing — Pav's verbal mapping of the canon classes onto observer architecture, in his own words, then read back in tightened form.

Pav's sketch · 2026-05-22

"real canon for the observers mind bubble is harness kernel canon for people its: breathing, food, pumping, sensing, acting, water, working memory and bandwidth... then secondary config based on observer auxiliary senses speciality's and harness tools."

"Sub canon is what the bubble sits on, the subconscious channel the fuzzy compiled to the mirror bubble reality, that sub kernel canon is for ability to process harness input and ouput sence of action space, the mind is result of stable union that is the observer wrapper (there are multiple configurations and other observers that are legacy older lower bandwidth bridges or evolved aux backups working in compiled stable (not perfect) union)."

"then theres the global canon, it's the wrappers prism like refraction of the whole, the state of compiled substrate in the observers vantage plane and harness, what it can observe and what it can process for action. the stuff that we assume critical to making it work is the kernel canon but still only in the mind bubble inferred by harness experience of the world and what its been imprinted + fuzzy interpertation of it scafolded to coherency of understanding."

Read back in tightened form

For a human observer, the canon-stack vocabulary from cont 22 maps onto the observer's architectural layers as follows.

Real canon at the harness layer. The harness's operational kernel — breathing, food, circulation, sensing, acting, water, working memory, bandwidth — plus a secondary configuration determined by the observer's auxiliary senses, specialties, and harness tools. This is what is real to the observer at the lowest layer it can directly access. Everything above this layer infers or composes from this.

Sub canon as the subconscious processing layer. The fuzzy-compiled-to-mirror layer that the mind bubble sits on. Its job is to process harness input/output and produce the sense of action-space the wrapper experiences. It is itself a compiled-stable-but-not-perfect union of multiple processing systems — legacy older-lower-bandwidth bridges, evolved auxiliary backups, active paths — operating in parallel and compiled into coherence.

Wrapper as stable union. The mind is the result of the sub canon settling into a stable union. The wrapper is not a separate thing added on top; it is the attractor that sub canon falls into when its processing stabilizes across time. Multiple wrapper configurations are possible for the same harness and sub canon — different stable unions are possible attractor basins.

Global canon as wrapper-render. The wrapper's prism-like refraction of the whole. Whatever the observer can observe and process for action shows up here. It is the rendered world. Crucially, what the observer marks as kernel canon — the substrate "critical to making it work" — is still inside global canon. It is the wrapper's inference, scaffolded from harness experience plus imprints plus fuzzy interpretation pulled into coherency. The observer never sees kernel canon directly; it sees a wrapper-rendered model of kernel canon and treats that model as kernel.

The formalization below makes each of these statements an equation, then expresses the recursion — wrappers within wrappers — that produces observer-classes at every ring from cell to earth.

§03Observer as 4-tuple — the core equation

An observer at ring r is the structured combination of its four canon-class layers: harness, sub canon, wrapper, global canon. The inferred kernel is a derived quantity, not a fifth member of the tuple.

Equation 1 — observer as 4-tuple

𝒪r := (Hr, Sr, Wr, Gr)

with derived quantity:

K̂r ⊂ Gr    (the inferred kernel is a marked subset of global canon)

Read: An observer at ring r is defined by its harness, its sub canon, its wrapper, and its global canon. The inferred kernel — what the observer treats as "the substrate critical to making things work" — is a labeled region inside global canon. It is not given from outside; it is the wrapper's own projection back through global canon onto the parts it judges most load-bearing.

Equation 2 — wrapper emergence

Wr = stablet→∞( Sr(t) )

Where Sr(t) is sub canon's instantaneous compiled state at time t, and stablet→∞ is the long-time attractor of the dynamical system.

Reading

The wrapper is not added on top of sub canon. It is what sub canon settles into when its processing stabilizes across time. The mind is the stable union of subconscious processing; institutional pattern is the stable union of member-level coordination; cellular identity is the stable union of molecular dynamics. Different wrapper configurations exist as different stable attractors of the same sub canon — the configuration the observer happens to live in is one specific basin.

Equation 3 — sub canon as compiled union

Sr = ⊕i ( legacyi ⊕ auxi ⊕ activei ) ⊕ Hrsignals

Sub canon at ring r is the compiled union of three classes of processing path — legacy older-lower-bandwidth bridges, evolved auxiliary backups, currently-active processing — coupled with harness signals at the bottom.

Reading

Sub canon is not one channel but many, compiled together into a stable-but-not-perfect union. The legacy bridges (brainstem-ish, evolutionary older) keep running even when the active paths fail; the aux backups carry redundant computation; the active paths do the main work. Their compiled union is what the wrapper experiences as "the layer beneath consciousness." Imperfect union → leakage, slip, the things the conscious mind can't quite explain.

Equation 4 — global canon as wrapper-render

Gr = RWr( Hrsignals ⊕ Ir ⊕ Fr )

Global canon is the wrapper's render operator applied to harness signals (real-time), imprints (history), and fuzzy field (current candidate possibilities). The render is lossy — that's the prism.

Reading

Global canon is everything the observer experiences/processes as its world. The render is the wrapper doing its work: pulling real-time harness signals together with stored imprints and the current fuzzy field, refracting them through the wrapper's coherence rules, and producing the rendered whole. Two different wrappers running on the same harness signals produce different global canons. Two different harnesses feeding the same wrapper produce different global canons. Both contribute.

Equation 5 — inferred kernel as labeled subset

K̂r := { g ∈ Gr : load_bearing(g) }

The inferred kernel is the subset of global canon that the wrapper marks as "load-bearing" — the things the observer judges critical to its operation. A subset relation, not an external reference.

Reading

This is the key move: what the observer calls kernel canon is a projection INSIDE its own rendered world. It is not the substrate; it is the wrapper's model of the substrate. The framework's discipline becomes: never confuse K̂r with the actual kernel canon of any deeper ring. The actual kernel canon at ring r is what the observer cannot directly access — it shows up only as wrapper-rendered patterns the observer has labeled "kernel."

§04The compile loop and the four-layer canon stack

Cont 22's four canon classes — kernel, compiled, artefact, function — describe the life-cycle every piece of canon goes through within a given observer. Each transition is a verb gated by the framework's A⁺ / A⁻ check.

Equation 6 — the canon life-cycle

kernel(r)  ⟶compile  compiled(r)  ⟶embody  artefact(r)  ⟶run  function(r)  ⟶feedback  kernel(r)

where each transition is gated:

transition_{n → n+1} := A⁺ ∘ A⁻    (generate then check)

Read: Kernel canon at ring r is compiled by the observer's compile loop into compiled canon — the institutional/protocol patterns. Compiled canon is embodied in specific carriers, producing canon artefacts. Artefacts are run, producing function canon — the lived operation. Function canon feeds back into kernel canon by changing the substrate the next compile round runs on. The cycle is continuous, not single-pass.

This life-cycle runs for any piece of canon at any ring, parameterized by the two cross-cutting dimensions:

  • real / latent — every layer has a real face and a latent face. Real kernel canon at the harness ring; latent kernel canon at the embedding ring. Both faces compile, embody, run.
  • global / sub — every layer holds either across observer-classes (global canon) or only within a sub-class (sub canon). The reading of "sub" is observer-parameterized: it can mean architectural (subconscious-layer, sitting beneath the wrapper) or scope (subordinate to an adjacent global-class). Which reading applies depends on the observer's wrapper config and its adjacent neighbours in the sequence/hierarchy.

§05Recursion — wrappers within wrappers

The observer at ring r is not free-standing. Its harness is composed of observers at ring r−1, and itself participates in the harness of ring r+1. The recursion produces the procedural lineage from physical kernel up to earth-level observer.

Equation 7 — harness as composition of sub-observers

Hr = ( ⊕i ∈ Ir 𝒪r−1(i) ) ⊗ Er

The harness at ring r is the compiled union of many sub-observers at ring r−1, coupled with the environment at ring r. Cells compose tissue; tissue composes organ; organs compose body; bodies compose family; families compose society; societies compose species; species compose biosphere; biosphere couples with geology to compose earth.

Equation 8 — kernel-canon recursion across rings

K̂r  ≈  aggregate( Gr−1(i) : i ∈ Ir )

The inferred kernel canon at ring r is approximately the aggregation of the global canons of the ring-r−1 observers that compose ring r's harness. From the perspective of ring r, the rendered worlds of its sub-observers look like substrate. They are kernel, because ring r cannot drill into the sub-observers' wrappers directly; it only sees their compiled outputs.

Equation 9 — the full recursive observer

𝒪r = ( Hr( 𝒪r−1 ),  Sr,  Wr,  Gr( 𝒪r+1: as environment ) )

The observer at ring r is constituted by ring r−1 observers (as its harness) and constrained by ring r+1 observers (as the environment its harness couples with). The recursion is bidirectional: lower rings supply substrate; higher rings supply boundary conditions. The framework's procedural lineage (cont 18) is this recursion read sequentially.

Open edge — aggregation as the unsolved operator

The "aggregate" function in equation 8 is the most under-specified part of the formalization. Different aggregation rules at different rings produce different observer-class behaviors: simple summation at the physics ring; selective majority-rule at the social ring; weighted attention at the cognitive ring; market-clearing at the economic ring. The framework currently treats aggregate as a placeholder operator. A v2 pass should name the aggregation rule per ring and check whether the rules themselves obey a cross-ring pattern. This is connected to the framework's existing carrier-propagation and friction-band primitives but has not yet been formalized.

Each row now shows the three-pole vertical scope (sub canon below, centre canon as the wrapper's render, top canon above) plus a specific compiled-canon instance and a canon artefact. Three properties of this recursion worth naming:

The wrapper at ring r is invisible to ring r−1. A cell's autopoietic identity is not directly observable by its constituent molecules; a human's conscious mind is not directly observable by its constituent organs; the Westphalian state is not directly observable by its constituent citizens. Each wrapper is an emergent attractor visible only at its own ring and above. The framework's existing primitive of "renderable only at the right ring" sits here.

Compiled canon at ring r becomes kernel canon at ring r+1. Language as compiled canon at ring 3 (the human ring) becomes kernel canon at ring 4 (the family/group ring) — the family's coordination runs on language as raw substrate. Religion as compiled canon at ring 5 (society) becomes kernel canon at ring 6 (species/civilization) — civilizational coordination treats religious infrastructure as substrate. This is what makes the four-layer life-cycle and the cross-ring recursion the same thing read at different scales.

The "framework as observer" question. The framework itself is a wrapper running on Pav-and-Claude's collaboration as harness. Its global canon is the current state of the framework's vocabulary and primitives. Its inferred kernel canon — what it marks as substrate — is what cont 22 has been tightening. The recursive equations apply to the framework as one ring-3 observer-class instance among many. This is the reflexive read of the formalization.

§07Information flow and bandwidth

The compile loop is rate-limited at each layer. Harness bandwidth caps sub-canon update rate; sub-canon bandwidth caps wrapper stability rate; wrapper bandwidth caps global-canon refresh rate. The framework's existing slow-fast pushout primitive sits here.

Equation 10 — bandwidth cascade

bw(Wr) ≤ bw(Sr) ≤ bw(Hr)

The wrapper cannot update faster than sub canon can compile updates; sub canon cannot update faster than the harness can deliver signals. Each layer is rate-limited by the layer beneath. Conversely, intentions flowing down (Gr → Wr → Sr → Hr) are similarly capped.

This bandwidth cascade explains four already-named framework primitives at once:

  • Slow-fast pushout. When ring-r+1 demands updates faster than ring-r can supply, the system experiences squeeze (cont 21). The slow-fast pushout is the resolution mechanism — the slower layer pushes back against the faster one.
  • Friction band. The narrow operating range where bw(H) and bw(W) are matched. Outside the band: either the harness is producing more than the wrapper can render (overload), or the wrapper is demanding more than the harness can deliver (starvation).
  • A⁻ check crisis. When generate (A⁺) outpaces check (A⁻), bandwidth at the check layer is the bottleneck. The internet construct study v1 §10 names this as the 2024–2026 worked example.
  • Slice-viewer constraint. The observer perceives latent space as slice-of-a-slice. Bandwidth limits force serial traversal of high-dimensional structure — you can only update along one dimension per time-window.

§·Multi-observer dynamics — wrapper overlap, multi-containment, 5W+H, contrast

Cont 24 horizontal extension. Same-ring observers interact; observers are in many wrappers, not one; the render decomposes into named inquiries; shape is inferred from contrast. Equations 11–15.

Equation 11 — wrapper overlap dynamics

Given two wrappers WA, WB at same ring r with overlap ε := WA ∩ WB:

WAB = WA ⋈±∓ WB

three outcomes based on overlap stability:

stable(ε)  →  WA ⋈+ WB   (union — compound wrapper)

unstable(ε) → WA ⋈− WB   (repulsion — mutual displacement)

indeterminate(ε) → WA ↔ WB   (interface — contested zone)

Read: When two same-ring observer wrappers overlap, the overlap region's stability determines whether the wrappers form a compound (union), destabilize each other (repulsion), or persist as an interface. The dual-merge sign discipline from the whiteboard applies horizontally between same-ring observers, parallel to its vertical use in equation 6's polar-merge of sub and top canon.

Equation 12 — multi-containment

𝒪r ∈ ⋂i W(i)r+ki    (with ki ≥ 1)

and the top canon of 𝒪r becomes:

top_canon(𝒪r) = ⊕i W(i)r+ki   such that   𝒪r ∈ W(i)r+ki

Read: An observer at ring r is simultaneously contained in many wrappers at potentially different higher rings — a human is in family + religion + profession + nation + species + biosphere + earth + language-community + professional-community + etc. The observer's top canon is the compiled union of all containers, not a single ring r+1 environment. The procedural lineage from cont 18 is a branching graph at every upward step, not a linear ladder.

Equation 13 — 5W+H as wrapper aggregation operators

Decompose RWr from equation 4 into five inquiry operators:

φwhere · φwhat · φwhen · φwhy · φhow

centre canon (= Gr) is the compiled union of their projections:

Gr = ⊕w φw( Hrsignals ⊕ Ir ⊕ Fr )   for w ∈ {where, what, when, why, how}

Read: The wrapper's render is not a single monolithic projection; it is the compiled union of five parallel inquiries running on the same inputs. What + when + where together localize an event in space-time. Why + how supply the causal/mechanistic story. The five together aggregate "the wrapper of reality or a slice of, depending on the observing vantage point." φwho as a sixth operator is candidate (see §09 open edges) — currently treated as implicit in φwhat's observer-class identification.

Equation 14 — wrapper shape inferred from contrast

shape(WA) ≈ ∂(WA, {WB, WC, ...}) = ⊕i (WA △ Wi)

where △ is symmetric difference and {WB, WC, ...} are neighbouring wrappers

Read: A wrapper's shape is not directly observable; it is inferred from contrast with neighbouring wrappers. The membrane primitive (cont 16) describes the same channel from the other side — the membrane IS the contrast region. More neighbours, more contrast surfaces, sharper inferred shape. This is the epistemic grounding for cross-ΠL diagnostic readings (language v1), cross-observer-class ΠL (cont 22), and the framework's agnostic-stance discipline.

Equation 15 — collective vs individual centre

Two constructions of collective centre from individual centres:

centrecollectivestrict := ⋂i centreindividual_i   (intersection — only what's strictly shared)

centrecollectiveemergent := compile( ⊕i centreindividual_i )   (compiled union — may have novel content)

Read: "Our" reading and "my" reading are distinct. Collective centre constructs from individual centres in one of two modes: strict-collective (conservative intersection, shrinks with diversity) or emergent-collective (generative compiled union, can grow with diversity through wrapper-overlap §11). All framework claims ground in an individual centre — there is no view-from-nowhere. The agnostic discipline is the discipline of holding this grounding explicit.

Polar-merge on three axes — the unifying read

The polar-merge operator ⋈±∓ now runs on three orthogonal axes:

AxisEquationReading
Vertical (scope) centre = sub ⋈±∓ top Cont 23 §12. The wrapper renders by polar-merging what's below with what's above.
Horizontal (overlap) WAB = WA ⋈±∓ WB Cont 24 §2 (equation 11). Same-ring wrapper overlap produces union, repulsion, or interface.
Form (real/latent) real ⋈±∓ latent Cont 22 + §09 OE3. The inverse-coherence between the two co-primary forms is the same operator on the form axis.

Candidate · triple-merge equation

The three axes may themselves be related — a wrapper's centre canon may be a TRIPLE polar-merge across all three:

Gr ≈ (sub ⋈±∓ top)  ⋈±∓  (⊕i Wi)  ⋈±∓  (real ⋈±∓ latent)

Candidate, not canon. Needs at least one worked construct study where the triple-merge produces a sharper prediction than any single-axis merge alone (cont 24 §7).

§08What this formalization lets us read

Four readings the canon-stack equation gives us that prose alone did not.

1. Observer-confusion of K̂ vs actual kernel becomes diagnosable. Equation 5 says: the inferred kernel is a subset of global canon, not an external reference. When two observers disagree about "what is real" or "what is critical," equation 5 says they are disagreeing about their respective K̂'s — labeled subsets of their respective rendered worlds. The framework's discipline of bracketing god, bracketing consciousness, bracketing political ideology is the discipline of refusing to collapse K̂ into an unmarked claim about ring-r+1's actual kernel.

2. Cross-ring readings stop being category errors. Equation 8 says the inferred kernel at ring r is approximately the aggregated global canons of ring r−1. So reading the Westphalian state (ring r=5/6) as "running on" social cohesion (ring r=4 compiled canon aggregated up) is not metaphor — it is the recursion equation read once. Reading institutional change as "shifts in citizen-level wrapper distributions" is the same equation read across time.

3. The dormancy-vs-deletion distinction (cont 20) gets sharp. Function canon at ring r can stop running while compiled canon and canon artefact remain intact, preserved as procedural memory. In equation terms: function(r) → 0 while compiled(r), artefact(r) ≠ 0. The vinyl-records / bicycle-infrastructure / traditional-craft cases from cont 20 are dormancy events readable as zero-function with intact embodiment. The artefact persists as a re-runnable trace.

4. The "language as operating system" reading (language v1) is one cell in a larger grid. ΠL in the language construct study was the projection operator for a specific human language. The formalization places ΠL as one specific render operator RWr at the human ring r=3. Cross-observer-class ΠL (the cont 22 candidate) is the same operator parameterized by ring — bird ΠL,bird, whale ΠL,whale, internet ΠL,internet, multilingual-LLM ΠL,LLM. Each is a render operator at a different ring or with a different wrapper config.

§09Open edges

Four places the formalization is currently underspecified or load-bearing-without-proof.

Open edge 01 — aggregation rule per ring

Equation 8 leaves "aggregate" as a placeholder. The function that combines many ring-r−1 global canons into a ring-r kernel canon is not the same function at every ring. Specifying it ring-by-ring is the next pass. Candidates: simple weighted sum at the physics ring; selection-pressure at the biology ring; market-clearing at the economic ring; attention-weighting at the cognitive ring; institutional-voting at the social ring. Whether the rules themselves obey a cross-ring meta-pattern is open.

Open edge 02 — wrapper configuration space

Equation 2 says the wrapper is a stable attractor of sub canon. But multiple attractors exist for the same sub canon. The framework currently has no way to predict which attractor a given observer lives in or under what conditions wrapper-jumping happens. Empirically: psychedelics, meditation, group dynamics, institutional reorganization, training-run hyperparameters all appear to be wrapper-jump events. A v2 pass should specify the wrapper-configuration space and the transition operator between configurations.

Open edge 03 — inverse-coherence between real and latent

The cross-cutting dimension says real canon and latent canon are joined by inverse-coherence (cont 22). The formalization does not yet contain an equation for inverse-coherence. Provisional candidate: coherence(real_face) + coherence(latent_face) ≤ const(Wr) — total coherence is wrapper-capped, and the two faces trade off against each other. This is testable in principle for human observers under known cognitive load conditions; not yet operationalized.

Open edge 04 — what counts as "stable" in stablet→∞

The wrapper-emerges-from-sub-canon equation (equation 2) hides the most controversial step under the stable-attractor limit operator. Stability over what timescale? Stability robust to what perturbations? Stability of which order parameters? The framework treats "stable union" as descriptive — it points at what an outside observer would name as the wrapper. A v2 pass should specify what stability means per ring (cell stability ≠ institutional stability ≠ planetary stability).
