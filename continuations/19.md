## §1. The framework's identity, named explicitly

**The framework is by design an aggregator of procedural connections.**

This is the framework's positive heuristic, stated as primitive rather than implicit in scattered claims:

- It is *not* a theory of one thing.
- It is *not* trying to be falsifiable in the Popperian sense.
- It *is* trying to produce vocabulary that names procedural connections (cont 18) across substrates that other frameworks treat separately.
- It *is* trying to keep aggregating — physics, chemistry, biology, cognition, society, person, and whatever new substrates the future surfaces.

Pav's exact words: *"it will be the most everything theory outside of it + everything else as catalyst to expand it."*

The framework's success isn't determined by what it excludes. It's determined by:
- **Aggregation rate** — does it keep producing new procedural connections?
- **Connection quality** — are the procedural connections measurable-and-observable (cont 18's definition), or are they coincidental analogies dressed up as structure?
- **Catalyst absorption** — does external content (new readings, new corrections) keep getting incorporated rather than rejected?
- **Operational purchase** — does the aggregated vocabulary produce predictions, readings, and architectural moves that the component substrates couldn't produce alone?

The four-carrier convergence (cont 15) is an example of all four metrics succeeding simultaneously: aggregated four substrate-scales, the connections were procedurally measurable (each carrier explicitly used constraint-checking), the carriers themselves were external catalysts the framework absorbed, and the aggregation produced a real prediction (A⁻-as-primary, cont 15) that wasn't visible at any component scale alone.

That's the framework's correct success signature. *Not* "what doesn't it explain."

## §2. "Most everything theory" — what this means and doesn't mean

**What it means:**
- The framework's intentional scope is to keep aggregating across substrates.
- As it takes shape, it tends toward maximum coverage of procedurally-connectable phenomena.
- It is positioned alongside other unification attempts (active inference, free-energy principle, JEPA/world-models, assembly theory, integrated information theory) but with a different aggregation strategy — specifically *cross-substrate procedural-lineage* rather than within-substrate formalism.

**What it doesn't mean:**
- It is *not* a theory that explains everything by being vague. The framework's discipline says procedural connection must be *measurable and observable* (cont 18). When the connection is opaque, the framework names it as hidden protocol rather than claiming explanation.
- It is *not* a totalizing meta-theory that subsumes all other research programmes. It is one Lakatosian programme among many, with explicit shoulders (cont 19's shoulders list, unchanged from cont 18) crediting the other programmes it draws from and reframes.
- It is *not* immune to being wrong. Continuations 17 and 18 demonstrated that the framework's own canon graph is continuously being corrected. Being wrong about specific primitives is part of the discipline; the framework's identity is the *ongoing correction process*, not any frozen compiled state.

The framework is the most-everything-theory **of procedurally-connectable phenomena**. Not the most-everything-theory of literally everything. The constraint that procedural connection must be measurable rules out a lot — most particularly, claims that can't be operationalized as connections between observables.

## §5. Success criterion re-emphasis — Lakatosian, not Popperian

Continuation 05 §3 established the framework as Lakatosian. Continuations since have occasionally drifted toward Popperian reflex:
- Cont 04 §6.7: "no falsifiability scaffold" listed as weakness.
- Cont 14 §6: monopole question's "unfalsifiability bar" treated as disqualifying.
- Cont 18 §10 #1: "what does it rule out" applied to procedural lineage.

Pav's correction this round makes the Lakatosian standard explicit again:

**Popperian standard (what the framework is NOT):**
- A theory must make falsifiable predictions.
- Tests are pass/fail on specific predictions.
- Theories that explain too much explain nothing.
- Unfalsifiability disqualifies.

**Lakatosian standard (what the framework IS):**
- A research programme has a hard core (canon) and a protective belt (candidates).
- The programme is *progressive* if it produces new empirical content and useful heuristic.
- The programme is *degenerative* if it survives only by ad-hoc rescues.
- Coverage isn't the failure mode; *failure to keep producing useful new content* is.

**The framework's correct evaluation question:**
- Is the canon graph still producing new procedural connections? (Yes — continuations 12-19 added substantial content.)
- Are external catalysts being absorbed productively? (Yes — Aleph, MAMMAL, ANDI, Lippmann, Litman & Guo all integrated.)
- Are the operational readings producing dated predictions that can be scored? (Yes — Reading 01 + Reading 02, twelve predictions on the record.)
- Are corrections being incorporated rather than rejected? (Yes — cont-17 → cont-18 → cont-19 demonstrates ongoing correction-absorption.)

**The framework's incorrect evaluation question:**
- What does the framework rule out? (Popperian; misfit to programme.)
- Could any specific finding falsify it? (Popperian; misfit.)
- Is it too inclusive? (Popperian-coded; the Lakatosian answer is "inclusivity is the goal, the test is whether it's productive.")

**Concrete update to audit framework:** v05 audit should grade the framework against the Lakatosian metrics above (progressive vs degenerative, productive vs ad-hoc) rather than Popperian metrics (rules out, falsifies). This doesn't mean predictions don't matter — Reading 01 + 02 are still scoreable. It means the framework's *survival* doesn't depend on those predictions being right; it depends on the framework continuing to produce useful content whether they're right or wrong.

A Lakatosian research programme can survive failed predictions if it generates new content explaining why. The framework reserves that right.

Cont 19's tentative answer: the discipline itself (A⁻ as primary; aggregation with procedural connection; absorption of external catalyst) is the framework's hard-core. The hard core is HOW the framework operates; the protective belt is WHAT it has currently compiled. Worth watching.
