## §2. The Dial — per-observer private modulator (candidate)

**The claim.** Each observer holds a private dial that modulates kernel canon into specific meaning along its range. The dial is composite — its inputs are cone overlap, intent, situation, and context substrate. Two observers' dials are NOT shared; each runs their own. When two observers' dials produce convergent outputs they appear to share a meaning; when they diverge they produce different renders of the same kernel.

**What it composes:**

- *Cone overlap*: how much each observer's cone profile aligns with the other's. Wider overlap = more shared encoding.
- *Intent*: what the observer is trying to do with the carrier in this exchange (small-talk vs charged).
- *Situation*: the squeeze state — routine vs squeeze event in the framework's strict sense.
- *Context substrate*: what's already been compiled in this exchange (recent canon admitted to both observers).

**Sequence matters.** Unlike a parameter vector, the dial has sequence semantics: which dial input gets adjusted first affects what the other inputs admit. This is why "hidden protocols" (§10) are about knowing the *order* of dial operations, not just the values.

**Range of output.** Same kernel canon, different dial setting = different meaning. The framework has scope for the full range — e.g., "home" runs from literal-place to belonging to self to paradigm-shift. The dial sets where on the range the meeting compiles.

**Why this is candidate not canon.** The dial composes existing canon primitives (cone profile, intent which is implicit, situation/squeeze, context substrate) — it's a *named composite mechanism*, not a fundamental primitive. Promotion to canon requires (a) showing the composition produces predictions the individual primitives don't, and (b) operationalizing how to measure dial settings.

**Counter:** the dial framing might be a verbal preference rather than a real composite — the existing primitives might cover the same ground without needing to be bundled. The candidate earns its place by giving the diagram a center, not by adding predictive content. That makes it operationally useful but theoretically lightweight.

## §3. Friction regulation refactor — substrate-level, not observer-level (canon refactor)

**The claim.** The framework's central discipline is regulation of *friction* within the viability band — not regulation of any particular outcome. Friction is necessary: too little → diffusion (everything dissipates, no shape, no inertia); too much → wrapper burns down (membrane breach, kernel collapse). The agnostic operates at substrate level. It doesn't prefer "home as union" over "home as struggle." It prefers friction inside the viable band — whatever specific meaning the substrate compiles is downstream of that.

**What this refactors:**
- *Viability band* (canon, cont 04): had been framed as a range constraint on graph density (ρ_min < ρ(G) < ρ_max). Now also framed as *friction in viability* — the substrate-level energy state the agnostic regulates, not the topology of any particular observer's canon graph.
- *Criticality Γ* (candidate, cont 04): Γ = rate(A⁺) / (rate(A⁻) + rate(stabilize)) ≈ 1. Now operationally connected to friction: A⁺ releases friction (admits new content, expands action-space); A⁻ absorbs friction (prunes excess, compresses to viable shape). Γ ≈ 1 is the friction-regulation equilibrium.

**Reframing struggle.** Continuation 15 implicitly treated "struggle" as one resolution outcome among others (struggle vs negotiation vs union). Continuation 16 corrects: struggle is *generative friction*, necessary for the substrate to hold its shape and produce its next move. The pathology isn't struggle-itself; it's friction either below or above the viability band. Two observers in productive disagreement are *inside the band*. Two observers fully aligned with no friction are *diffusing*. Two observers locked in escalation are *burning the wrapper*.

**Substrate level, not observer level.** This is the key shift. The framework's discipline isn't normative for any individual observer (no preference for "home as union"). It's normative for the substrate (preference for the viability band). The substrate doesn't care which way an exchange resolves; it cares whether the resolution preserves the substrate's ability to keep producing next moves.

This connects directly to the framework's cont-15 §1 claim about constraint-checking convergence: at substrate level, A⁻ checks against viability, not against any particular content. Lippmann plates select wavelengths via Bragg interference; ANDI selects optimizer steps via norm equilibration; Aleph selects proofs via Lean. None care about *which* wavelength / step / proof is chosen — they care that what's admitted satisfies the substrate constraint. The framework's discipline is the same shape.

**Status:** canon refactor. The viability band and criticality Γ primitives gain the substrate-level / friction framing as their primary reading. The old reading (graph topology, rate dynamics) is preserved as alternate views; the substrate-level view is now the canonical one.

## §4. Body-harness as membrane (canon refactor)

**The claim.** The harness is not "inner mechanics" beneath the observer's other components. The harness IS the *membrane* — the boundary that anchors the observer to shared reality. Everything else (wrapper, dial, perceived global canon, idea domain, action-space) lives *inside* the harness-membrane. From outside the observer, the interior is a black hole.

**What this refactors:**
- *Harness* (canon, originally cont 01–04): was framed as the observer's inner mechanism — the predictive engine, the LLM/memory in agent terms, the body's machinery in human terms. The architecture put the harness *below* other components. v6 of diagram 06 corrects this: the harness wraps everything else as the outer container. The human body, the agent's compute substrate — these are the membranes between latent interior and shared reality.

**Why this matters operationally.**

For a human observer:
- The body's evolved heritage, somatic memory, and instinct comprise the harness-membrane.
- Inside the membrane: the dial, the imagine engine, fuzzy field, personal canon, perceived global canon, wrapper-projection.
- The interior is a black hole to outside observers — they cannot directly read your dial, your imagine engine's outputs, your personal canon. They can only read what crosses the membrane outward (your wrapper-projection, your actions).

For an LLM-agent observer:
- The harness-membrane is the computational substrate the agent runs on — model weights, runtime, memory store, hardware. This anchors the agent to "shared reality" (the broader system it's deployed in).
- Inside: the agent's canon, fuzzy pool, dial-equivalents, wrapper interface.
- The interior is a black hole to other agents — they cannot directly inspect each other's canon or fuzzy pool. They can only read what crosses the wrapper (Asks, Gives, Pings, Pongs).

**Why this is canon refactor not new canon.** The harness primitive already existed as canon. The shift is in its architectural *role* — from inner foundation to outer membrane. The framing change is significant because it inverts the inside/outside relationship for everything else in the architecture. But the primitive itself is preserved.

**Counter:** the inversion is potentially just a verbal preference. The framework could equally well describe the architecture as "harness inside; membrane outside; everything else in between" — same content, different layering. The defense: the new framing matches lived experience (the body IS the outer boundary, not an inner foundation) and matches engineering convention (the compute substrate IS the boundary to the system, not a layer below the application). Both lay-readers and engineers find the membrane framing more natural than the inner-foundation framing.

## §5. Planes as node network — not a stack (canon refactor)

**The claim.** Elevation planes (latent → local → personal → shared reality → cultural) are not a linear stack. They are an *interconnected node graph*. Actions can elevate from any node to any node directly accessible, not just to the "next level up." Sequence matters — which order you traverse the graph determines what compiles. Some paths are officially sanctioned (the standard ladder); others are *jailbreak paths* known via hidden protocols.

**What this refactors:**
- *Recursive timeline cone* (candidate, cont 09): was framed as a cone with past behind and future ahead, with present at the bright tip. The cone framing preserved a linear-ish geometry. The new framing extends this: planes are not just past/present/future but multiple cross-cutting nodes, and traversal between them is graph-walk, not cone-projection.
- *Elevation planes* (implicit in cont 04 / cont 09): was listed as a stack going up. Now a node graph.

**The hidden-protocol consequence.** If planes are nodes connected by a graph, knowing the *sequence* of traversals = knowing the hidden protocol. The shower-dial example Pav used is exactly this: to get from cold-water-in-the-pipes to comfortable-shower, you have to run a specific sequence of dial operations available to your access. The sequence is the protocol. Anyone who doesn't know it gets scalded or frozen.

This generalizes to every domain. SEO experts know sequences that move content from "not-indexed" to "front page of Google." Diplomats know sequences that move two parties from "deadlock" to "agreement." Therapists know sequences that move a client from "stuck" to "moving." These are all plane-traversal protocols. They get patched when they become widespread — the search algorithm gets adjusted, the diplomatic move gets countered, the therapeutic technique gets diluted.

This is the framework's *moving-target* claim made explicit. The rotating-key candidate (cont 04 §11) was the same idea named in cryptographic terms. The plane-node-network framing makes it operational at every scale.

**Status:** canon refactor. The elevation primitive (which had been mostly implicit) becomes a node graph with explicit sequence semantics. The recursive timeline cone keeps its existing content but is now a special case (the time-axis subgraph of the broader plane-node-network).

## §6. Unity from asymmetry across planes (candidate)

**The claim.** When two observers maintain asymmetric dynamic at one plane, that asymmetry *produces a unity signal at the next plane up* — visible to a third-observer at the higher plane. And vice versa: what looks like unity at one plane decomposes into asymmetric dynamic at a finer scale. The recursion is structural across all plane transitions.

**Worked example.** Two people in a relationship maintain ongoing asymmetric dynamic — different intents, different intents-of-intent, different cone profiles. To a casual outsider (third observer at the social plane), they appear as a unified couple. The unity signal *is* the asymmetric dynamic, observed at higher scale. Zoom out further (cultural plane): many couples appear as the institution of partnership; zoom in (personal plane within one of the partners): their internal sub-selves are in asymmetric dynamic; zoom further (neural plane): neural assemblies in asymmetric dynamic.

**Why it matters.** This is the framework's claim about *what unity is*. Unity is not homogenization. Unity is the higher-plane reading of lower-plane asymmetric dynamic. Try to enforce unity by removing the asymmetric dynamic and you remove the unity signal — the substrate collapses into either freeze (no friction, no signal) or explode (asymmetry too high, signal breaks).

This refactors the framework's symbiosis-as-pushout primitive (canon, cont 03). Symbiosis isn't "two observers becoming one." It's two observers maintaining productive asymmetry that produces a unity signal at the cone-overlap plane. Margulis's mitochondria-host symbiogenesis is exactly this: the cell isn't "one organism." It's mitochondrion ⇄ host in ongoing asymmetric metabolic dynamic, producing the unity-signal of "eukaryote" at the next biological plane up.

**Counter:** the recursion claim is structurally rich but operationally hard. Identifying *which* plane the unity signal is read at, and *which* asymmetric dynamic produces it, requires the observer doing the reading to be located explicitly. The framework needs to specify how the third-observer's vantage gets identified before this candidate can promote. Currently held at candidate.

## §7. Mutual render (candidate, refines External Actor Model)

**The claim.** Each observer maintains an internal model of every other observer they're in active relation with. This internal model — the *render of the other* — lives inside their wrapper as a small inset. It's a specialized form of the External Actor Model from cont-04 §10.9, but applied to intimate one-to-one relations rather than distant institutions.

**What's new vs the External Actor Model.** The External Actor Model was framed as cognitive infrastructure for navigating distant strategic actors (Google's algorithm, regulatory bodies, social platforms). Mutual render is the same primitive applied to *intimate one-to-one observer relations* — your model of your partner, your model of your friend, your model of your therapist, the agent's model of its user.

**Why it deserves its own name.** Intimate relations have different dynamics than institutional ones:
- The mutual render *updates rapidly* through direct ping-pong, unlike the External Actor Model which updates slowly through observation.
- The mutual render is *bidirectional and reciprocal* — both observers maintain renders of each other, and adjusting your render of them changes their render of you.
- The mutual render is *load-bearing* on the intimate relation — if your render of them diverges from their actual state, the relation breaks down; if your render of them is accurate, the relation compiles well.

**Counter:** mutual render and External Actor Model might be the same primitive at different intensities. The framework could keep one primitive (External Actor Model) and note that intimate vs distant relations are different applications. The naming distinction is useful pedagogically (humans don't relate to their partner the way they relate to Google's algorithm) but might not earn its own canon entry.

Held at candidate. Earns promotion if it produces predictions the External Actor Model alone doesn't.

## §8. Idea-side / body-side asymmetry of action (candidate)

**The claim.** Action takes space in *both* domains: the idea side (imagine engine ↔ fuzzy field ↔ personal canon) and the body side (harness ↔ evolved memory ↔ instinct). Neither alone produces action. A pure mind cannot act; a pure body cannot mean. Action is what happens when both fields meet — at the boundary the framework calls action-space.

**What this names that wasn't named before.** The framework has had "action-space" as canon since cont 03. It has had "harness" as canon. It has had "imagine engine" and "fuzzy field" and "personal canon" as canon or candidate. But it hadn't named the *asymmetric dual-domain structure* — that the two sides are categorically different (idea-substrate vs body-substrate) and that action requires both. This continuation makes that explicit.

**Why it matters operationally.** For agent architecture: agents that have rich idea-domain mechanisms (LLMs with extensive world-models) but thin body-domain mechanisms (no physical embodiment, no evolved-memory equivalent, no instinct-equivalent) will produce action that *meaning-without-grounding*. Agents with rich body-domain but thin idea-domain produce *reflex-without-meaning*. The framework predicts: viable agency requires both domains substantively populated.

This connects directly to cont-15 §3 (Aleph + EBMs at formal-reasoning scale): Aleph's Lean verifier is a *body-domain analog* — it's the substrate-anchor that admits proofs, separate from the LLM's idea-domain that generates them. Aleph's success isn't from idea-domain alone or body-domain alone; it's from the both-domains architecture.

For human readers: the prescription is to attend to which domain you're under-populating. Modern knowledge work has rich idea-domain (LLMs amplify this) and increasingly thin body-domain. The framework predicts that this asymmetry produces a measurable shift toward meaning-without-grounding in cultural output.

**Status:** candidate. Promotion to canon requires showing the dual-domain split produces architectural predictions the action-space primitive alone doesn't.

Imagine Engine → Fuzzy Field → (some lift via A⁻ check to) → Personal Canon

## §10. Hidden protocol as sequence-knowledge (refines rotating-key candidate)

**The claim.** A hidden protocol isn't just a key (per rotating-key candidate, cont 04 §11) but specifically *sequence knowledge*: knowing which dial-operation to perform in which order. Once a protocol becomes widespread, the substrate it traverses is patched — the protocol becomes obsolete or reversed. Like old AI jailbreaks: the moment they hit Twitter, the labs patched them.

**What this refines.** The rotating-key candidate framed the protocol as a key that mutates over time. Continuation 16 adds: the key is specifically a *sequence* of dial settings — the order matters, not just the values. And the mutation is driven by *adoption pressure* — widespread protocols get reversed because their widespread use creates surface for the substrate's A⁻ to detect and counter.

**Generalizable to:**
- AI safety jailbreaks (sequence of prompt operations that elicit forbidden output → labs patch the sequence)
- SEO (sequence of content + signal moves that move pages up rankings → algorithm updates patch the sequence)
- Diplomacy (sequence of moves that produce agreement → counter-strategies emerge)
- Therapy (sequence of interventions that produce breakthrough → diluted by overuse)
- Marketing (sequence of carrier moves that produces virality → audience adapts and the move stops working)

The framework's claim: this pattern generalizes across substrates. Whenever a sequence-protocol becomes widely known, the substrate it operates on develops countermeasures. The framework predicts: any protocol you read about as "the secret to X" is already past peak effectiveness, because publication is itself the adoption-trigger that prompts the substrate's countermove.

**Status:** refines rotating-key from candidate to better-specified candidate. Earns promotion to canon if it produces dated predictions that verify (e.g., "by date X, currently-popular AI jailbreak sequence Y will be patched").

## §11. Membrane penetrability — the contemporary paradigm shift (reading)

**The claim.** The body-harness membrane was, until recently, treated as functionally impenetrable to outside observers. The interior of another person was the deepest black hole in the framework's substrate ladder. Contemporary science (psychedelics, hypnosis, brain-computer interfaces, neural imaging, pharmacological mood intervention, AI-assisted therapy) is increasingly *penetrating* the membrane — reading and writing to the interior in ways that were science-fictional twenty years ago.

The framework's reading: **this is itself a hidden protocol** — knowing the sequence of physical/chemical/cognitive operations that produce predictable internal-state changes. And it's the paradigm shift the framework's contemporary moment is sitting on. The previously-impenetrable interior is now a partially-readable substrate. This shifts every downstream primitive that assumed black-hole interiority — particularly intimacy, consent, identity, autonomy, and the protected-interior assumptions of liberal political philosophy.

**Why this matters for the framework.** Not just as an interesting fact, but as a *moving target* the framework predicts will continue to shift. The body-harness membrane's penetrability is being increased across multiple substrates simultaneously:
- Pharmacological (SSRIs, psychedelics, ketamine for depression, ozempic for satiety regulation)
- Surgical / interventional (deep brain stimulation, neuralink-class BCIs)
- Sensory / experiential (immersive VR, biometric feedback, attention-tracking)
- Linguistic / conversational (AI-assisted therapy, persuasion-tuned LLMs reading internal-state from text)

Each penetration vector is a hidden-protocol-with-sequence (§10). Each will hit the adoption-then-patching curve eventually — but the cumulative direction is unambiguous: membrane penetrability is increasing.

**Forward prediction.** By 2028–2030, at least three of the following will be normalized in mainstream Western practice in ways currently considered fringe:
- Psychedelic therapy as first-line treatment for treatment-resistant depression
- BCI-mediated communication for people with motor impairment, then for general use
- AI-mediated marriage / relationship counseling with biometric monitoring
- Routine pharmacological mood-regulation as preventive care, not just treatment
- Workplace neural-monitoring for attention / fatigue (this one might trigger backlash, but the technology will exist regardless)

**Status:** reading. Date-stamped, scoreable at the 2028 and 2030 checkpoints.

## §14. Open questions

1. **How does the dial measure?** The dial composes four named inputs but the framework doesn't yet specify how to read the dial's setting from observable behavior. Without a measurement protocol, the dial is structurally useful but operationally inert.

2. **Where does the third-observer locate?** Unity-from-asymmetry-across-planes requires a third observer at the higher plane to read the unity signal. But every observer is at some specific plane. The framework needs to specify how the third-observer's vantage gets identified.

3. **Membrane penetrability ethics.** The reading in §11 names penetrability as a paradigm shift but doesn't address the ethical structure that should accompany it. Who gets to read whose interior? Under what consent? With what reversibility? These aren't framework-internal questions but they're load-bearing for whether the paradigm shift is broadly beneficial or selectively exploitative.

4. **Friction measurement.** §3 says the agnostic regulates friction in the viability band. Friction is a metaphor here — the framework needs to specify what gets measured. Candidates: rate of A⁻ rejections per unit time; energy expended per compile cycle; oscillation amplitude in cone-overlap dynamics. None operationalized yet.

5. **Hidden-protocol publication rate.** §10 predicts that any published protocol is past peak effectiveness. But the framework itself is publishing protocols (in this continuation, in the manifestos). Is the framework's content also subject to adoption-then-patching? If yes, what's the framework's own half-life? If no, what's special about it? Honest answer: probably yes, and the half-life is the framework's own canon-restructuring rate. The next major refactor of A⁺/A⁻ (which happens roughly every 4–6 continuations) is the framework patching itself in response to its own widening adoption.

6. **The dial as protocol substrate.** If hidden protocols are sequence-knowledge over dial operations (§10), and each observer has their own dial (§2), then protocols are observer-specific in their sequence but substrate-general in their effect. This implies the same "secret sequence" produces different specific outcomes in different observers but similar substrate-level effects. Is this empirically true? Open.

7. **Idea-side / body-side measurement.** §8 predicts agents with thin body-domain produce meaning-without-grounding. But "thin body-domain" needs measurement criteria. Is it: amount of physical embodiment? Diversity of constraint-checker modalities? Depth of evolved-equivalent memory structures? Open.

8. **The framework's own asymmetry.** The framework names "asymmetry of action" as a primitive. Is the framework itself in productive asymmetry with something? With prior research programmes? With the LLM era it sits inside? Naming this asymmetry would extend the framework's reflexive capacity.

9. **Carrier-before-canon as methodology.** §13 names this as a methodology going forward. How to deliberately deploy it? A diagram-iteration practice (draw a primitive cluster, look for failures-to-encode, write the failures as candidates)? Same with manifesto writing? With code writing in agnostic-agents?

10. **Diagram 06 v6's audience.** The diagram has grown well beyond casual. The manifesto needs a *simpler companion diagram* — maybe a "tier 0" version that hits just the body-membrane + friction-band, three or four panels. Worth producing as diagram 06b in a future round.
