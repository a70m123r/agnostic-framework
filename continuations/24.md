## §2 Wrapper overlap dynamics — union vs repulsion

When two observer wrappers at the same ring r overlap, the overlap region ε := W_A ∩ W_B has a dynamic of its own. Three outcomes:

- **Stable overlap → union.** The wrappers form a compound wrapper W_AB = W_A ⋈<sup>+</sup> W_B with shared centre canon. New attractor basin, larger than either wrapper alone. Examples: two scientific schools merging into a unified field; two states forming a federation; two cells fusing into a syncytium.

- **Unstable overlap → repulsion.** The wrappers destabilize each other in the overlap region: W_A ⋈<sup>−</sup> W_B → mutual displacement. Each wrapper's stability requires the other to move out of the overlap. Examples: two competing religious orthodoxies in the same territory; two startup founders with incompatible visions; two cancers crowding each other in the same tissue niche.

- **Indeterminate overlap → interface.** The overlap region is neither stable nor destabilizing; it persists as a contested zone. W_A ↔ W_B with no merge and no expulsion. Examples: cultural border regions; bilingual communities; multi-stakeholder negotiations.

The structural equation:

> **W_AB = W_A ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> W_B**

is the whiteboard's dual-merge sign discipline applied HORIZONTALLY between same-ring observers. Cont 23 §12 used the same operator vertically (centre = sub ⋈±∓ top); cont 24 lifts it horizontally too. The framework now has the polar-merge operator running on two axes; §7 unifies them under a single operator-on-three-axes reading.

**What gets promoted to canon:** the wrapper overlap dynamics with three outcome types (union, repulsion, interface) and the horizontal polar-merge equation.

**Falsifiers:** an observed wrapper overlap that produces a fourth outcome type incompatible with union / repulsion / interface; or an overlap that systematically violates the dual-merge sign discipline (e.g., compatible signs producing repulsion not union).

## §3 Multi-containment — an observer in many wrappers, not one

The cont 23 recursion treated each observer at ring r as contained in a single ring r+1 environment. That is wrong. An observer is contained simultaneously in MANY ring r+1 wrappers.

A human observer at ring r=3 is contained in:
- A family wrapper (ring 4)
- A religion wrapper (ring 5)
- A profession wrapper (ring 5)
- A nation wrapper (ring 5/6)
- A language community wrapper (ring 5/6)
- A species wrapper (ring 6)
- A biosphere wrapper (ring 7)
- A planetary system wrapper (ring 8)

These are not on a single linear hierarchy. Some are at the same ring as each other (religion and profession are both ring 5); some span rings (language community sits between ring 5 and ring 6). The containment graph is many-to-many across rings, not a simple ladder.

The structural equation:

> **𝒪<sub>r</sub> ∈ ⋂<sub>i</sub> W<sup>(i)</sup><sub>r+k_i</sub>** &nbsp; (with each container at potentially different ring k_i ≥ r+1)

And the observer's top canon becomes the compiled union of all containers that hold it:

> **top_canon(𝒪<sub>r</sub>) = ⊕<sub>i</sub> W<sup>(i)</sup><sub>r+k_i</sub>** &nbsp; such that &nbsp; 𝒪<sub>r</sub> ∈ W<sup>(i)</sup><sub>r+k_i</sub>

The framework's procedural lineage (cont 18) was a useful first pass but read too linearly — physics → chemistry → biology → cognition → society → person. The actual containment graph branches: a person is in family AND religion AND profession AND nation all at the same level, plus species and biosphere and earth at higher levels. The recursion equation 7 from cont 23 (H<sub>r</sub> = ⊕ 𝒪<sub>r−1</sub>) was right for harness composition; equation 8 (K̂<sub>r</sub> ≈ aggregate(G<sub>r−1</sub>)) was right for kernel inference. But the implicit assumption that there is a single ring r+1 above was wrong.

**What gets promoted to canon:** multi-containment as structural, with top canon as the compiled union of all containers. The procedural lineage becomes a branching graph, not a ladder.

**Falsifiers:** an observer demonstrably contained in only one ring r+1 wrapper and no others, with no overlap-based participation in other wrappers. Hard to find for any complex observer; suggests the canon move is well-grounded.

## §4 The 5W+H as wrapper aggregation operators

Cont 23 equation 4 said G<sub>r</sub> = R<sub>W<sub>r</sub></sub>(H_signals ⊕ I<sub>r</sub> ⊕ F<sub>r</sub>) — global canon (now centre canon) is the wrapper's render of harness signals, imprints, and fuzzy field. The render operator R<sub>W</sub> was treated as a single projection.

Pav's sketch decomposes R<sub>W</sub> into named inquiry operators. The wrapper aggregates centre canon by running five (possibly six, with Who) questions across its inputs:

- **φ<sub>where</sub>** — spatial localization. Where in the substrate does this signal originate? Where in the action-space does this intention land?
- **φ<sub>what</sub>** — identification. What is this? What category of observer / signal / artefact?
- **φ<sub>when</sub>** — temporal localization. When did this happen, when will it happen, when is it relevant?
- **φ<sub>why</sub>** — causal / intentional inference. Why is this happening? What's the upstream cause or downstream purpose?
- **φ<sub>how</sub>** — mechanism inference. How does this work? How is this connected to other things?

(φ<sub>who</sub> is sometimes added — identity attribution. Pav's sketch listed five, not six; treating Who as implicit in observer-class identification under φ<sub>what</sub>, but the framework should hold open the option to add it explicitly as a sixth operator if construct studies show it's load-bearing separately.)

The render operator decomposes:

> **G<sub>r</sub> = R<sub>W<sub>r</sub></sub>(inputs) = ⊕<sub>w</sub> φ<sub>w</sub>(H_signals ⊕ I<sub>r</sub> ⊕ F<sub>r</sub>)** &nbsp; where w ∈ {where, what, when, why, how}

Centre canon is the compiled union of the five inquiry projections. The wrapper isn't doing one big render; it's running five parallel inquiries and compiling their answers into a coherent picture. "What" + "when" + "where" together localize an event in space-time. "Why" + "how" together provide the causal/mechanistic story. The five together aggregate "the wrapper of reality or a slice of, depending on the observing vantage point" — Pav's phrasing.

The 5W+H are not unique to the framework — they're the journalism / detective / scientific-inquiry framing that has been standard for centuries. The framework's move is to identify them as the WRAPPER OPERATIONS that decompose the previously-monolithic render. This makes R<sub>W</sub> explicit instead of mystery-box.

**What gets promoted to canon:** the 5W+H as the wrapper's decomposed inquiry operators that aggregate centre canon. R<sub>W</sub> is no longer monolithic.

**Falsifiers:** a wrapper that demonstrably renders centre canon without running any of the five inquiries; or a sixth inquiry operator load-bearing across many observer-classes that the framework has missed. (Who is the candidate sixth; framework holds it as candidate pending worked examples.)

## §5 Difference / contrast as the diagnostic for wrapper shape

Wrappers cannot be observed directly. Their shape is inferred from contrast with neighbouring wrappers. Pav's phrasing: "the rings or layers of difference / contrast is where the shape and scope of the wrappers can be inferd."

The structural claim: the only way to read a wrapper's boundary is by what's on the other side of it. A single wrapper considered alone has no shape — shape only emerges in contrast. Two wrappers next to each other reveal each other's boundaries simultaneously.

The structural equation:

> **shape(W<sub>A</sub>) ≈ ∂(W<sub>A</sub>, {W<sub>B</sub>, W<sub>C</sub>, ...}) = ⊕<sub>i</sub> (W<sub>A</sub> △ W<sub>i</sub>)**

where △ is symmetric difference. The shape of W<sub>A</sub> is the union of its symmetric differences with each neighbouring wrapper. More neighbours, more contrast surfaces, sharper shape.

This is the same channel the framework's membrane primitive (cont 16) describes from the other side. The membrane IS the contrast region. The "rings or layers of difference / contrast" Pav names are the framework's existing membrane stack at the wrapper-overlap scale. What cont 24 adds: the EPISTEMIC implication — wrapper shape is always inferential, never directly observed.

This explains several existing framework primitives in one move:

- **Why language v1's cross-Π<sub>L</sub> projections work as diagnostic.** Comparing the same event rendered through English-OS, Chinese-OS, and Russian-OS surfaces each OS's wrapper shape by contrast.
- **Why the framework's agnostic-stance discipline matters.** We can't see a wrapper directly; bracketing claims about wrapper-content lets contrast do the diagnostic work without commitment.
- **Why cross-observer-class Π<sub>L</sub> (cont 22 candidate) is high-leverage.** Reading whale / bird / AI / internet wrappers in contrast with the human wrapper is the most direct way to read the human wrapper's specific shape.
- **Why difference between cultures is generative, not just frictional.** Contrast is where wrapper shape becomes legible. Cultures with high contrast can read each other better than cultures with low contrast, even though contrast also produces friction.

**What gets promoted to canon:** wrapper shape as inferential-via-contrast, not directly observable. The membrane primitive (cont 16) is the same channel; cont 24 names its epistemic implication.

**Falsifiers:** a direct observation of a wrapper's shape that doesn't go through contrast with neighbouring wrappers. The framework treats this as principled-impossible — any observation of a wrapper is itself a wrapper-internal render (equation 5: K̂ ⊂ centre canon), and that render relies on contrast operations.

## §6 Collective vs individual centre — the my / our distinction

Pav's sketch closes with the precise note: "from our or in the very true sense my reading of the compiled from my collctive centre point." Two distinct constructions:

- **Individual centre.** The single observer's centre canon — the wrapper's prism refraction. Pav's "in the very true sense, my" reading. This is the ground from which any reading happens.

- **Collective centre.** The shared centre canon of a group of observers. Pav's "our" reading. This is constructed in one of two ways:

> **Strict-collective:** &nbsp; centre<sub>collective</sub> := ⋂<sub>i</sub> centre<sub>individual_i</sub> &nbsp; (only what's strictly shared across all individuals)
>
> **Emergent-collective:** &nbsp; centre<sub>collective</sub> := compile(⊕<sub>i</sub> centre<sub>individual_i</sub>) &nbsp; (the compiled union — may have novel content not present in any individual)

These are different. The strict-collective is conservative (only intersection) and shrinks with diversity. The emergent-collective is generative (compiled union) and can grow with diversity through wrapper-overlap dynamics (§2 union). The framework treats both as canon; which one is operative in a given case depends on the wrapper-overlap configuration.

Crucially: **all readings ground in an individual centre.** Pav's "in the very true sense, my reading" is structural, not just rhetorical. There is no view-from-nowhere. The framework itself — as one wrapper running on Pav-and-Claude collaboration — has its own individual centre that grounds every claim it makes. The "agnostic" in agnostic framework is the discipline of holding this individual-centre grounding explicit, not pretending to a collective-centre that doesn't exist.

The recursion gets richer: an individual observer is contained in many wrappers (§3 multi-containment); each of those container-wrappers has its own collective centre constructed from its constituent individual centres; the individual's top canon is the compiled union of those collective centres; and those collective centres are themselves observers whose centres are constructed by their own collective dynamics. Recursion all the way up, with collective-centres at every ring.

**What gets promoted to canon:** the individual-vs-collective centre distinction, with two construction modes (strict-collective and emergent-collective). The framework's claims are always grounded in an individual centre — explicitly named.

**Falsifiers:** a claim demonstrably made from no individual centre (view-from-nowhere). The framework treats this as impossible — every reading happens FROM somewhere.

## §7 Polar-merge as the framework's unifying operator — three axes, one structure

The polar-merge operator ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> introduced at the whiteboard, lifted vertically in cont 23 §12, and lifted horizontally in cont 24 §2 now runs on three axes:

1. **Vertical axis** (cont 23 §12) — centre canon = sub canon ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> top canon. The wrapper renders by polar-merging what's below with what's above.

2. **Horizontal axis** (cont 24 §2) — W<sub>AB</sub> = W<sub>A</sub> ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> W<sub>B</sub>. Same-ring wrapper overlap produces union, repulsion, or interface depending on the sign-discipline of the merge.

3. **Form axis** (cont 22 + open edge 03) — real canon ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> latent canon = the wrapper's coherent reading. The inverse-coherence between real and latent that cont 22 named is the polar-merge on the form axis.

One operator, three readings. The framework now has a single load-bearing operator that runs on all three orthogonal dimensions: vertical (scope), horizontal (overlap), form (real/latent). This is the closest thing the framework has yet found to a single mathematical move that captures its central claim — that compile-into-coherence happens via polar-merging opposite poles, and the merge can be productive (union, generative coherence) or unproductive (repulsion, friction) depending on sign-discipline.

A speculative tightening: the three axes may themselves be related. A wrapper's vertical sub/top, its horizontal overlap with neighbour wrappers, and its real/latent form all merge into the single coherent centre canon the wrapper lives by. If so, the wrapper's render is a TRIPLE polar-merge:

> **G<sub>r</sub> ≈ (sub ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> top) ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> (⊕<sub>i</sub> W<sub>i</sub>) ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> (real ⋈<sup>±</sup><sub>∓</sub> latent)**

This is candidate-level, not canon. Needs at least one worked construct study where the triple-merge reading produces a sharper prediction than any single-axis merge alone.

**What gets promoted to canon:** the polar-merge operator as load-bearing on three orthogonal axes.

**What stays candidate:** the triple-merge equation above.

## §8 Open edges

Four places cont 24 leaves work for a v2 pass.

### Open edge 01 — sign discipline for wrapper overlap outcomes

Cont 24 §2 says union/repulsion/interface depend on the sign discipline of the polar-merge but doesn't specify what determines the sign in any given overlap. Candidates: compatibility of compiled canons in the overlap region (stable signs → union, contradictory signs → repulsion); shared substrate availability (enough for both → union, scarce → repulsion); historical residue (positive prior contact → union, negative → repulsion). Needs operationalization per observer-class.

### Open edge 02 — branching factor of multi-containment

Cont 24 §3 says an observer is in many wrappers but doesn't specify how many or how to enumerate them. A human is in family + religion + profession + nation + language community + species + biosphere + earth; that's eight. Is eight typical? Is there a bound? Does the branching factor differ systematically by observer-class (a cell may be in fewer wrappers; a state may be in more)? Needs empirical investigation.

### Open edge 03 — the Who question

Cont 24 §4 lists five W's (where, what, when, why, how) per Pav's sketch. Journalism convention adds Who as the sixth. Framework holds Who as candidate — pending worked examples showing it's structurally distinct from φ<sub>what</sub>'s observer-class identification function. Construct studies of multi-actor interactions (e.g., the internet construct study v1's actor-fingerprint reading) may surface Who as load-bearing separately.

### Open edge 04 — collective centre construction mode selection

Cont 24 §6 names two constructions (strict-collective and emergent-collective) without specifying when each applies. Likely candidate: emergent-collective dominates when wrapper-overlap dynamics produce union (stable shared centre with novel content); strict-collective dominates when overlap is unstable (only the intersection survives the friction). But this is a hypothesis, not a derivation. Needs operationalization.
