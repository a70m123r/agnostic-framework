### 1.1 The originating intuition

**User quote**

**Steer captured**

The framework's existing position on dark matter is conservative and load-bearing: dark matter is gravitationally inferred, not directly observed; should be treated as hidden scaffold coefficient, not resource inventory; belief does not create mass (file 1 §6.3, §7.1). That position holds.

This round adds three specific candidate hypotheses about what kind of hidden scaffold dark matter might be, held in suspension as possibility-paths (which is itself §2 of this file applied reflexively to a cosmological question).

### 1.2 Three candidate framings

| Framing | What it would imply | Prior art with resonance |
|---|---|---|
| **Dormant DNA** | Dark matter is to baryonic matter as non-coding DNA is to coding DNA: present, structurally significant via gravitational effects, not currently "expressed" in the visible/active sense. Possibly regulatory (galactic rotation curves), possibly vestigial, possibly latent for future expression | ~98% of human DNA is non-coding; some regulatory, some structural, some genuinely unused. | **Intelligence force** | The substrate exhibits selection-like dynamics at cosmic scales without requiring those dynamics to be conscious. Dark matter could be the gravitational signature of large-scale information-organizing processes | Walker & Cronin's assembly theory (selection as physical signature); Smart-matter or computronium speculation; Wheeler's "it from bit" |

### 1.3 Discipline required

The first two framings are framework-permitted speculation: they extend the existing "hidden scaffold coefficient" position without committing to a specific ontology. They should be carried in the speculative-layer with the same labels as the rest of file 1 §5.3.

The third framing — **intelligence force** — sits at the slope toward Teilhard's Omega Point and new-age "consciousness is fundamental" framings. The framework's commitment to *agnosticism about consciousness* (file 1 §6.6) is precisely what keeps it from sliding down this slope. The disciplined version of the framing is admissible: **selection-like dynamics at cosmic scales without requiring conscious agency**. The undisciplined version — *dark matter is mind* — is not. Keep the first; resist the second.

### 1.4 The structural observation underneath

What unifies the three framings, and what makes the move framework-relevant rather than merely cosmological speculation:

> **From any local observer's vantage, parts of the substrate are too large, too old, or too dimensionally remote to render directly. We infer them through boundary effects. Dark matter is one such boundary effect.**

The framework's vocabulary lets the observer maintain multiple candidate hypotheses about what those boundary effects imply without forcing commitment. The three framings above are three deposited possibility-paths about a single fuzzy region (the nature of dark matter), held in suspension because no scout has returned definitive data. This is the epistemic stance the framework demands: don't compile to canon prematurely; don't dismiss; hold multiple readings in weighted suspension and let scouts return data over time. Which leads directly to §2.

### 1.5 The "infinite inflation expanding into other dimensions" subclaim

Connects to continuation 05 §5's cosmic remap. The cosmic expansion never stopped; it propagated through substrates of increasing structural complexity. The user's extension here: the expansion may also be propagating into *dimensions the local observer cannot render*. Dark matter could be the boundary effect of that dimensionally-extended expansion.

This is consistent with the framework's existing claim that "fuzzy is the edge of our collective rendered universe" (file 1 P15). The unrendered substrate continues to expand whether or not we can detect it. Dark matter as the gravitational shadow of that continuing expansion is one possible framing. Speculative-layer, but coherent with the framework's existing logic.

## 2. Multi-possibility deposition mechanic

### 2.1 The originating intuition

**User quote**

> when an agnostic hist a fuzzy it doesn't just bounce back it lays multiple possibility's and possibility's to scout later

**Steer captured**

This is a real refinement to the framework's core action mechanic. The standard model in continuations 02–07 was: agnostic encounters fuzzy plank → runs probe → receives give → compiles or doesn't. Single probe, synchronous resolution.

The refinement: agnostic encounters fuzzy region → **deposits N candidate possibility-paths** → each becomes a scoutable target with its own lifecycle. Multiple, asynchronous, weighted.

### 2.2 The change to framework mechanics

| Aspect | Standard model (cont. 02–07) | Multi-possibility version (this round) |
|---|---|---|
| Fuzzy encounter | One probe, one give | One encounter, N candidate planks deposited |
| Action mechanic | Probe-and-receive (synchronous) | Deposit-and-defer (asynchronous) |
| Update timing | Canon updates at probe time | Possibilities sit in suspension until scouts return |
| Scout role | Generic explorer of fuzzy | Targeted investigator of specific deposited possibilities |
| Data model | Single canon-fuzzy boundary | Pool of weighted candidate possibilities with lifecycles |
| Attention | Implicit | Explicit allocation across the possibility pool |

### 2.3 Prior art with structural resonance

| Existing mechanic | What it shares with multi-possibility deposition |
|---|---|
| **Beam search in language models** | Multiple candidate trajectories maintained, pruned as evidence accumulates. The exact mechanic at smaller scale |
| **Many-worlds quantum branching** | Every measurement deposits all outcomes; local observation collapses one branch but others continue |
| **Bayesian model averaging** | Multiple hypotheses maintained with weighted probabilities; updated over time as data arrives |
| **Active inference with competing models** | Friston's framework already permits multiple generative models with weights |
| **Backtracking search in AI** | Explore one branch, return to alternatives if it fails |
| **Exaptation in biology** | Biosphere maintains under-utilized features that become useful in different selection regimes — the genome is a possibility-deposition mechanism on evolutionary timescales |
| **Real options theory in finance** | Hold options open until forced to choose; the value of suspension itself is calculable |
| **Speculative execution in CPUs** | Modern processors deposit multiple computation paths speculatively; the unused ones are discarded once the outcome is known |

These aren't analogies. They are *operational implementations of the same mechanic* at different scales and substrates. The framework now has a clear lineage for what it's doing.

### 2.4 Integration with continuation 06 and 07

The wavelet weight from continuation 07 gets a new and load-bearing role. The **freshness** dimension becomes operational:

```text
deposit(fuzzy_region, observer_context) →
  generate N candidate planks {p₁, p₂, ..., pₙ}
  for each pᵢ:
    initialize wavelet weight (magnitude, spin, scale, freshness=now)
    enqueue in scout-target pool with priority = magnitude × novelty / age
  return possibility pool reference

scout_cycle(pool, budget) →
  select top-k planks by priority
  for each selected pₖ:
    run probe; receive give
    update wavelet weight (magnitude updated, freshness=now)
    if updated_magnitude > τ_compile: compile to canon
    if updated_magnitude < τ_prune: drop
    else: re-enqueue with new priority
  age all other planks (decrement freshness)
  drop any with age > τ_max
```

This gives the framework concrete machinery for what previously was qualitative:

- **Attention allocation** becomes calculable (top-k by priority, bounded by budget)
- **Decay** becomes explicit (freshness decrements over time)
- **Pool size** becomes a viability constraint (too small → freeze; too large → explode; the "do not freeze, do not explode" kernel canon from continuation 03 becomes computable as a pool-size band)
- **Compile/prune thresholds** become parameters of the system (τ_compile, τ_prune, τ_max)

### 2.5 Implication for external-actor modeling

The §10.9 audit ExternalActor model needs a structural change. The standard implementation (one model per actor, updated over time) is wrong for the framework's own logic. The correct implementation:

```yaml
ExternalActor:
  id: string
  candidate_models:                # NOT a single model
    - model_id: string
      harness: object
      wrapper: object
      weight: WaveletWeight
      last_scouted: timestamp
      compile_confidence: float
    - ...
  scout_queue: list[model_id]      # scheduled investigations
  consolidated_view: object        # weighted aggregate (read-only derivation)
  last_observed_at: timestamp
```

When the agent encounters a new external actor, it deposits multiple candidate models (e.g., "Google is in pruning-dominant phase," "Google is in connect-dominant phase but pre-announcing pruning," "Google is performing a strategic feint"). Scouts return data over time; weights update; some models compile to canon, others get pruned, some persist in suspension.

This is structurally how serious analysts already work — they hold multiple competing hypotheses about an actor's intent and update with new data. The framework now names this and gives it operational machinery.

### 2.6 Why this matters operationally

Three real consequences:

1. **The framework now has explicit attention machinery.** Continuation 07 introduced freshness as a wavelet dimension; this round makes it load-bearing. Possibilities decay; attention is bounded; pool dynamics are computable.

2. **Asynchronous canon update is now the default.** Previously the framework implied synchronous probe-and-resolve cycles. Now the canon updates whenever a scout returns useful data, which can be at any time. This is closer to how real cognition and real institutions actually work.

3. **The reading engine from continuation 05 §6.1 becomes much sharper.** Instead of "maintain a model of Google and update with new data," it becomes "maintain a pool of candidate models of Google with weights and a scout queue; update pool dynamics as data arrives." The latter is much more honest about what the agent actually knows and doesn't know.
