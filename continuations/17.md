## §1. The discipline being practiced

The framework has named A⁻ as the primary canon-formation operator (cont 15). It has not until now run A⁻ deliberately on its own previously-admitted canon. That asymmetry was an oversight — the same discipline the framework claims is load-bearing in agent systems and physical substrates wasn't being applied to the framework's own structural inventory.

Continuation 17 corrects this. The pattern is:

1. **Audit identifies at-risk primitives** — v04 §3 went through cont 12–16 systematically.
2. **For each at-risk primitive, three options:**
   - **Demote to pruned** if the primitive hasn't earned operational value and probably won't.
   - **Fold into adjacent canon** if the primitive duplicates or is a special case of existing canon.
   - **Keep with sharpening** if the primitive's at-risk status reflects a missing connection or worked example that can be supplied without re-scouting.
3. **The pruned section keeps the demoted primitives** with explicit provenance — what was tried, why it didn't earn place. The framework's claim about pruning honestly applies to the framework's own moves.

Doing this round publicly (in the continuations file, on the live site) is itself a discipline move. A framework that prunes only when forced is operating on different epistemics than one that prunes deliberately as practice.

The going-forward rhythm v17 establishes: every 4–6 continuations of expansion, one round of housekeeping. Not every round needs to grow.

## §2. Demotions

### 2.1 Spiral substrate cycle (continuation 12 §1.2)

**Original claim:** substrate emits format; format elaborates through nested intermediates; elaboration returns to substrate with new compile capacity, not new fuzzy. The cycle is spiral rather than closed.

**Why demoting:** the framework has used this primitive zero times since continuation 12. It hasn't connected operationally to any prediction, agent-architecture move, or reading. The structural shape (spiral cycle returning enriched) is suggestive but the framework treats it as decorative — names it once, doesn't reach for it.

**v04 assessment:** at risk.
**v17 decision:** demote to pruned. Preserve as a deposited possibility-path that didn't compile.

**What's preserved:** the *idea* that substrate-format relationships involve enrichment-on-return is not gone — it lives implicitly in the recursive timeline cone primitive (cont 09) and in the compile-cycle's general claim about A⁻ admitting enriched candidates. The framework didn't need a separate spiral-substrate-cycle entry for any of that.

### 2.2 Compile-capacity enrichment (continuation 12 §1.3)

**Original claim:** what the spiral substrate cycle returns with is not new fuzzy but new compile capacity — new restructuring sense, more compression, faster compile rate.

**Why demoting:** paired with spiral substrate cycle in cont 12 and shares the same fate. Used once, then quiet. The core insight (observers can gain capacity-to-compile, not just content-to-compile) is real and worth preserving, but it doesn't need its own primitive — the framework's existing canon already handles capacity differences via cone profile (cont 04) and harness primitives.

**v17 decision:** demote to pruned, paired with spiral substrate cycle. The two were always a unit.

### 2.3 Monopole question / observer-dependence of distinction (continuation 14 §6)

**Original claim:** Pav's refinement of the A⁺/A⁻ monopole open question. The question whether monopoles can propagate from latent (where they exist) to phenotype (this level of reality), AND whether the categorical distinction A⁺/A⁻ is itself observer-imposed.

**Why demoting:** the candidate was structurally rich but recursively about its own answerability. Cont 14 itself flagged the unfalsifiability risk. The "escape hatch" (an observer-naive observation of phenotype-stable categorical asymmetry) has not been operationalized in the four months since deposit. v04 flagged it.

**v17 decision:** demote to pruned. With important caveats — see below.

**What's preserved:** the framework's broader observer-dependence-of-distinction claim is *not* being pruned. The point that "drawing a categorical line is itself an observer move" remains true and shows up across multiple primitives (kernel canon as shared latent that observers carve into meaning via the dial; the A⁻ check as inherently observer-positioned). What's being pruned is the *specific monopole-question candidate primitive* — not the philosophical observation behind it.

This is the trickiest of the three demotions. The framework's epistemic discipline says preserve what was tried, including hard questions. The monopole question goes to pruned not because it's unimportant but because *it doesn't function as a primitive*. It's better preserved as an open philosophical question in the framework's discussions of A⁺/A⁻ than as its own canon entry.

## §3. Folds

A fold is different from a demote. Demoted primitives go to the pruned section with explicit "didn't earn place" reasoning. Folded primitives keep their *content* by being absorbed into an adjacent canon entry that gets sharpened in the process. The fold trades one canon node for a richer adjacent node.

### 3.1 Procedural canon (muscle-memory layer) — folded into body-domain

**Original claim (continuation 13 §4.2):** the implicit, non-articulable, body-level knowledge that operates below conscious access. The muscle-memory of language, gesture, physical skill, social rhythm.

**Why fold:** continuation 16's body-domain primitive (with evolved + memory + instinct as components) covers the same territory. Procedural canon is structurally what cont 16 called "body-side canon" — the canon that lives in the harness-membrane rather than in the personal canon graph.

**Fold action:** the body-domain primitive's "evolved · memory · instinct" framing gains "procedural canon" as the proper name for the body-side canon. The cont-13 procedural canon entry is removed from PRIMITIVES; the cont-16 body-domain entry is updated to include the fold.

**What this resolves:** the audit v04 noted "procedural canon appears to overlap with body-domain without clear differentiation." The fold makes the overlap explicit and gives the body-domain its named canon-component.

### 3.2 Engineered emergence — folded into emergent garden

**Original claim (continuation 13 §4.1):** designing systems where emergence is intentionally sought and tuned (vs avoided or unmanaged).

**Why fold:** the emergent garden primitive (cont 13 §3) already captures the discipline — build the rules and building blocks; nurture the system; observe what comes up. "Engineered emergence" is the *intentional-mode* of emergent garden practice (deliberately setting up conditions for desired emergent behavior). The unintentional-mode would be "letting it happen and seeing what grows."

**Fold action:** emergent garden gets a sub-distinction added — *intentional mode* (engineered emergence) and *receptive mode* (observing what comes up). The cont-13 engineered emergence entry is removed from PRIMITIVES; emergent garden absorbs the content.

**What this resolves:** the audit v04's "used twice then quiet" concern. Engineered emergence wasn't earning a separate canon spot because it was already an aspect of a more general primitive.

### 3.3 Mutual render — folded into External Actor Model

**Original claim (continuation 16 §7):** each observer's internal model of every other observer they're in active relation with. A specialized form of the External Actor Model from cont-04 §10.9 applied to intimate one-to-one relations.

**Why fold:** v04 §2 flagged this. The mutual render primitive *itself acknowledged* it might be the External Actor Model with a distance parameter. The fold makes the relationship explicit.

**Fold action:** the External Actor Model gains a *distance-parameter* explicit acknowledgment — intimate-distance (high update rate, bidirectional, load-bearing on the relation) vs distant-distance (slow update, mostly unidirectional, navigation-relevant). The cont-16 mutual render entry is removed from PRIMITIVES; External Actor Model absorbs it.

**What this resolves:** the framework had two adjacent primitives with overlapping content. Now one richer primitive with named distance modes.

## §4. Keep-with-sharpening

These two primitives were at-risk per v04 but survive after being given the connecting structure that earned their place.

### 4.1 Slice-viewer constraint — connected to planes-as-nodes

**Original claim (continuation 14 §3):** the observer perceives latent space as a slice-of-a-slice-of-a-slice, holding most dimensions fixed and walking one at a time. Mathematical form of the harness/wrapper limit.

**Why at-risk:** cont 14 named it as candidate; no further development since. v04 noted no operational content.

**Sharpening:** continuation 16's planes-as-nodes refactor (canon) makes slice-viewer suddenly operational. The reason observers traverse the plane-node-network *one node at a time in sequence* (rather than experiencing all nodes simultaneously) is precisely because they are slice-viewers. The hidden-protocol-as-sequence-knowledge claim (cont 16 §10) requires slice-viewer constraint as its substrate — protocols are sequence-knowledge only because observers are slice-locked.

So slice-viewer isn't redundant; it's *the perceptual constraint underneath planes-as-nodes*. The fold doesn't happen — instead, both primitives remain but slice-viewer gets a connecting note saying "the perceptual reason planes-as-nodes traversal is sequence-bound rather than parallel."

**v17 decision:** keep slice-viewer as candidate with new edge to planes-as-nodes. Sharpened, not demoted.

### 4.2 Holographic wrapper — awaiting agnostic-agents v0.2

**Original claim (continuation 15 §6):** wrapper instantiated as a Lippmann-style Bragg-reflection layer that selectively transmits content based on receiving observer's cone angle.

**Why at-risk:** v04 noted "interesting architecture spec but no implementation has been attempted."

**Sharpening:** the framework's discipline says some candidates can only be checked by building. Holographic wrapper is one of those — it's a buildable specification that becomes testable when agnostic-agents reaches v0.2 (reference Python implementation, multi-agent ask/give loop). The candidate's at-risk status reflects *blocked-by-engineering-work*, not *intrinsically-decorative*.

**v17 decision:** keep holographic wrapper as candidate with explicit "next-test" criterion — when agnostic-agents v0.2 implementation is built, test whether a Bragg-style wrapper produces measurably different multi-observer behavior than a uniform-projection wrapper. Until then, hold without further development.

This sharpening is different from §4.1's. Slice-viewer was sharpened by connecting to existing canon; holographic wrapper is sharpened by being given an explicit *gating condition* for next assessment. Both stay as candidates; both have clearer status now.
