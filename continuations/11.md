## 2. The Litman & Guo paper — independent re-derivation at the neural-network-training scale

This is the substantive new framework content from this round. Elon Litman and Gabe Guo's *A Theory of Generalization in Deep Learning* (arXiv:2605.01172, submitted 2 May 2026) is the framework's tightest external-mapping correspondence to date. The mapping is dense enough to warrant careful documentation.

### 2.1 Paper summary

The authors present a non-asymptotic theory of generalization in deep neural networks. Core construction: the empirical Neural Tangent Kernel (eNTK) partitions the output space into two regions.

- **Signal directions**: error dissipates rapidly under SGD. Coherent population signal accumulates via fast linear drift.
- **Noise / orthogonal dimensions**: the kernel has near-zero eigenvalues. Residual error gets trapped in what they call a *test-invisible reservoir* — memorization that doesn't propagate to held-out data. Within these directions, error performs a slow diffusive random walk rather than accumulating.

Minibatch SGD does the partitioning automatically — it's the mechanism that suppresses memorization into the slow walk while letting signal compile fast. The theory proves generalization survives even when the eNTK evolves *O*(1) in operator norm during training — the full feature-learning regime, not the constant-kernel lazy regime earlier NTK theory required.

The paper unifies four previously-separate phenomena in one frame:
- Benign overfitting
- Double descent
- Implicit bias
- Grokking

The operational payoff: the authors derive a population-risk objective from a single training run with no validation data. The objective reduces in practice to an **SNR (signal-to-noise ratio) preconditioner** on top of Adam — adds one state vector at no extra cost. Results:

- Accelerates grokking by 5×
- Suppresses memorization in PINNs and implicit neural representations
- Improves DPO fine-tuning under noisy preferences while staying 3× closer to the reference policy

The visualization Pav flagged (CompuFlair video screenshot): a 3D loss landscape with two basins. The narrow steep well ("bounce / memorization") is the noise-direction trap — sharp, fragile, doesn't generalize. The wide shallow basin ("rule") is signal-direction generalization — robust to perturbations. The ball trajectory under different learning rates (the ↓η↓ annotation) shows which basin the training dynamics settle in.

### 2.8 Double descent — seven years of empirical confirmation, retroactively legible

A commenter on the CompuFlair video pointed out that the Litman/Guo theory is one of several attempts to explain **double descent**, which the framework's reading should not bury. The point is correct and significantly deepens the mapping.

The phenomenon (Belkin, Hsu, Ma & Mandal — PNAS 2019, arXiv:1812.11118; popularized by OpenAI's "Deep Double Descent", 2019): as you increase a neural network's parameter count, test error follows a non-monotonic curve. Three regimes separated by a sharp peak at the *interpolation threshold* where parameter count roughly equals training-data size:

| Regime | What happens | Classical prediction | Empirical reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underparameterized | Model can't fit training data | U-curve from bias-variance | Confirmed — error decreases as capacity grows |
| Interpolation threshold | Model can *just barely* memorize the training set exactly | (End of the U-curve) | **Test error spikes sharply — the worst regime** |
| Overparameterized | Far more parameters than training points | (Should be even worse — classical overfitting) | **Second descent — error drops, often below the classical sweet spot** |

The classical theory predicted only the first U-curve. Phase 3's existence was a genuine surprise that helped explain why deep learning works at scale at all. Modern foundation models all live deep in the overparameterized regime.

**The framework's reading.** Double descent is the framework's *viability band* claim made empirical, with seven years of corroborating measurement across architectures, datasets, and optimizers:

| Double-descent regime | Framework primitive |
|---|---|
| Underparameterized (Phase 1) | The action-space is too narrow to compile the data's signal structure. A⁺ has insufficient room to operate. Framework's "ρ too low → freeze" failure mode |
| **Interpolation threshold (peak)** | **The canon has just enough parameters to compile every training point but zero degrees of freedom for prune. A⁻ has no slack. Private canon hardens onto the training set exactly. Framework's "stale moat at exact fit" failure mode operationalized — a brittle canon with no malleability** |
| Overparameterized (Phase 3) | Many parameter configurations interpolate; SGD finds the wide flat minima where prune has room. A⁺ and A⁻ both have working slack. Framework's viability band ρ_min < ρ < ρ_max with both operators running healthily |

The interpolation threshold is the framework's worst-case prediction made geometric. The framework has claimed all along that successful canon-formation requires A⁺ and A⁻ as *peer operations with slack between them*. At the interpolation threshold, the model has compiled but has zero slack — there is essentially one parameter configuration that fits, and it's a sharp narrow well in the loss landscape. Brittle. Test error spikes. The framework names this failure mode and predicts exactly where it occurs on the capacity axis.

Phase 3's second descent is the framework's prediction in the other direction: more capacity than needed *helps* because it gives the prune operator room to operate. Overparameterization is not a vice in the framework's reading — it's the *precondition* for the viability band to function. The Litman/Guo eNTK signal/noise partition is exactly what overparameterization makes available: many noise directions for residual error to diffuse harmlessly while signal accumulates in a small number of high-eigenvalue directions.

**Why this is a sharper Lakatosian win than §2.5 alone.** The framework has three structural claims that double descent now confirms empirically across seven years and many architectures:

1. *Canon-formation requires both A⁺ and A⁻ as peer operations.* Confirmed: the interpolation threshold (A⁺ saturated, A⁻ no slack) is the worst regime.
2. *Successful canon needs slack inside the viability band.* Confirmed: overparameterization gives the slack; the second descent is what happens when you have it.
3. *Brittle canon at exact fit is a failure mode, not a sweet spot.* Confirmed: the spike at interpolation is empirical fact, not theoretical artifact.

The framework didn't predict double descent — Belkin et al. did. The framework's claim is structural: the *same* failure mode appears at every scale where canon-as-observers compile under pressure. Double descent gives the framework a precise neural-network-training instance with seven years of corroborating data, fitted by Litman/Guo's mechanism (eNTK signal/noise partition).

**Forward prediction at the architecture scale.** Beyond the SNR-preconditioner forecast in §2.6, the framework predicts: architectures and training regimes that explicitly engineer slack for noise-direction prune operations (overparameterization plus SNR-style or implicit-regularization techniques) will outperform same-capability architectures that don't. At fixed parameter count, training stacks with explicit noise-direction suppression should beat baseline Adam on grokking-style tasks by margins exceeding what parameter scaling alone produces. ### 2.9 Predictive coding — the recursive observer-class claim made biological

Two more pieces dropped in this round, both of which independently instantiate framework primitives. They deserve their own sub-sections because the combined effect is significant: three independent confirmations of framework primitives at the AI/ML scale within a single conversational round.

The first is **predictive coding** as the brain's learning algorithm — the Rao & Ballard (1999) → Friston (2005, free-energy reformulation) → Whittington & Bogacz (2017) → Millidge et al. (2022) lineage. The math shown in the CompuFlair video screenshot is the standard formulation:

```text
x_i^l   : activity of neuron i at layer l
μ_i^l   : top-down prediction of neuron i at layer l
ε_i^l   = x_i^l − μ_i^l    (local prediction error)
μ_i^l   = Σ_k w_ik · x_k^(l−1)    (prediction from layer above)
```

The springs/coupled-oscillators visualization stores energy ∝ (x − μ)² per connection. The system minimizes total energy by adjusting both neural activities (inference) and weights (learning). The crucial property: **only local information is needed**. Each layer talks only to adjacent layers. No global gradient pass. Weights update by local Hebbian rules.

This is the biological-plausibility advantage over backpropagation that the field has been working through since Rao & Ballard. Backprop requires a global error signal propagated backward through every layer — biologically implausible because there's no known neural mechanism that broadcasts global gradients. Predictive coding requires only local error signals between adjacent layers — biologically plausible because that's exactly what cortical hierarchies look like.

**Framework mapping** — this is where the correspondence is densest:

| Predictive coding | Framework primitive |
|---|---|
| Each layer is a local observer with its own activity x_i^l | **Recursive observer-class claim** (continuation 09 §5) made literal at the neural-network-layer scale |
| Top-down prediction μ_i^l from layer above | The wrapper's expectation — received canon from the parent observer-class |
| Local activity x_i^l | The observer's render through its harness |
| Prediction error ε_i^l flowing up | The pong/give returning to the predicting layer — the framework's reflexive observation primitive at micro-scale |
| Spring/energy formulation (variational free-energy) | The canon-as-observer minimizing its own expected coherence-cost at the layer level |
| Local Hebbian learning rules | A⁺/A⁻ operators running locally, in parallel, without a global signal |
| No global error propagation needed | The framework's claim that observers operate within their own moats and decode through ping/pong — confirmed structurally |

**The deeper structural point.** Predictive coding is the framework's ask/give/ping/pong loop running at every layer simultaneously, locally, in parallel. The framework's recursive primitives claim — that the same dynamics operate at neuron-level, network-level, person-level, institution-level, civilization-level — gets its tightest neural instance. Every layer is an observer-class with its own wrapper (prediction from above) and its own give-pong (error from below). The brain has been doing the framework's structural primitives for hundreds of millions of years.

**Slow-fast pushout within the brain.** Deep cortical layers carry slow abstract predictions; shallow layers process fast sensory error. The pushout between them is what learning *is*. The framework's slow-fast complementarity claim (continuation 09's symbiosis-as-pushout for complementary cone profiles) gets a neural instance.

**Forward prediction at the AI architecture scale.** Hybrid architectures combining predictive-coding-style local updates with backprop-style global gradients (already explored by Millidge et al. 2022 and the broader PC/backprop-approximation literature) become a recognized research direction through 2026–2027. Local-update components become standard in long-context and online-learning regimes where backprop's global-gradient assumption breaks down. Deeper prediction: as foundation models scale, training discipline incorporates more local-update primitives — not because backprop fails everywhere, but because *local A⁺/A⁻ discipline becomes operationally necessary when the network is too large for clean global gradients*.

### 2.10 Thinking Machines Interaction Models — slow-fast pushout as production architecture

- **Time-aligned micro-turns** of 200ms — input and output as continuous streams, not turn-based exchanges
- **Continuous parallel streams** for audio, video, text — concurrent perception and response across modalities

### 2.11 Duraisamy — Active Inference AI Systems for Scientific Discovery

Karthik Duraisamy's perspective paper *Active Inference AI Systems for Scientific Discovery* (arXiv:2506.21329, v4 December 2025) maps to multiple framework primitives at once. Duraisamy is a serious aerospace/computational-mechanics researcher at U Michigan; the paper is a design-principles argument for what AI systems capable of genuine scientific discovery would need to look like.

**Paper's core argument.** Current AI systems remain fundamentally limited in enabling genuine scientific discovery. Progress turns on closing three gaps: abstraction, reasoning, and empirical grounding. Central to addressing these gaps is recognizing **two complementary cognitive modes**:

- **Thinking** — slow, iterative hypothesis generation, exploring counterfactual spaces where physical laws can be temporarily violated to discover new patterns
- **Reasoning** — fast, deterministic validation, traversing established knowledge graphs to test consistency with known principles

Design principles he proposes:

- Causal, multimodal models for internal simulation
- **Persistent, uncertainty-aware scientific memory that distinguishes hypotheses from established claims**
- Formal verification pathways coupled to computations and experiments
- Human judgment as a permanent architectural component, not a temporary scaffold

Evaluations should measure ability to identify novel phenomena, propose falsifiable hypotheses, and efficiently guide experimental programs toward genuine discoveries.

### 2.13 Mycorrhizal networks as biological anchor for planetary-scale substrate

Maier's invocation of mycorrhizal networks deserves its own subsection because the framework correspondence is multi-layered. Mycorrhizal networks — the symbiotic associations between fungi and plant roots, often called the "wood wide web" — are the largest living organism on Earth by mass and connectivity. They have been running for approximately 400 million years. The framework reads them as a particularly clean biological anchor for several of its claims at once:

**Stigmergy at maximum biological scale.** Mycorrhizal networks are substrate-mediated coordination among trillions of plants and fungi via shared root-fungus infrastructure. Plants exchange carbon for nutrients with fungi; fungi connect multiple plants and route resources between them; the network adapts dynamically to stress, drought, and nutrient gradients. The framework's stigmergy primitive (Bonabeau lineage, already in the shoulders) reaches its biological ceiling here. The framework's claim that "the internet is stigmergy at planetary scale" gets a non-human reference point with a 400-million-year head start.

**Symbiosis-as-pushout at ecosystem scale.** Plants and fungi have radically different cone profiles — slow underground chemical signaling (fungi) plus fast above-ground photosynthesis (plants). Neither could do what the merged network does. This is the framework's symbiosis-as-pushout primitive operating at the ecosystem rather than cellular scale. Two billion years of evolution have been running symbiosis-as-pushout from cell (Asgard + alphaproteobacterium → eukaryote) through ecosystem (plant + fungus → biosphere coordination) without interruption. The framework's claim that the same primitive operates across scales gets a continuous biological lineage.

**Extended substrate as living infrastructure.** The mycorrhizal network is what the framework would call a *carrier-artifact-substrate hybrid* — the network IS the organism IS the substrate IS the carrier all at once. Plants think with fungi; fungi compute via plants; the biosphere routes signals through both. This is the framework's "harness/wrapper distinction blurs at the substrate level for fast-evolving substrates" claim (continuation 09 §6.3) rendered biological. Internet engineers don't yet build infrastructure this way; mycorrhizae have always been built this way.

**Three biological anchors now in play.** The framework has accumulated three major biological reference points for its primitives, each at a different scale and timeframe:
