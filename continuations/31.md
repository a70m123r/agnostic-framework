## §2 The ecological vocabulary that maps cleanly

Biology has worked vocabulary for the kind of selection dynamics the candidate doc was groping toward. Five pieces transfer cleanly:

### §2.1 Mimicry types (Pav's "mimics")

Biology distinguishes at least four mimicry strategies, each with a structural signature:

**Batesian mimicry.** A harmless species evolves the warning signal of a dangerous species. The mimic gets predator-avoidance without bearing the cost of being actually dangerous. Structurally: an actor presents the externally-rendered features of a high-cost-to-fake category without paying the actual cost. *Substrate signature:* the predator (= substrate's selection mechanism) cannot perfectly distinguish, so mimics fitness-equivalent to genuine.

**Müllerian mimicry.** Multiple harmful species converge on a shared warning signal. The signal becomes mutually-reinforcing. Structurally: multiple adversarial actors with distinct origins converge on shared messaging to amplify each other's signal. *Substrate signature:* the converged signal is harder to identify-and-resist than any individual signal because it appears to come from many independent sources.

**Aggressive mimicry.** A predator mimics the prey's signal to lure the prey in. Structurally: an extractor presents the cues of a beneficial-encounter to draw target into vulnerable position. *Substrate signature:* the lure is calibrated to target's actual preference-landscape; victim's normal preference becomes the attack vector.

**Automimicry.** Mimicry within species — one organism presents itself as multiple distinct organisms. Structurally: one actor maintains multiple distinct-looking aliases, each appearing independent. *Substrate signature:* the substrate's signal-aggregation mechanism (vote-counting, consensus-detection, multiple-source-agreement) gets exploited because it cannot detect that "multiple sources" all originate from one actor.

These are not metaphors borrowed for vibes. They are evolved strategies with mathematical formal-game-theoretic foundations (signaling games, Lotka-Volterra coevolution, fitness-equivalence under partial information). The framework's existing wrapper-overlap dynamics (cont 25) already have the substrate-with-partial-information structure; mimicry types name the evolved-strategy class that exploits that partial information.

**Candidate doc mapping:** Batesian + automimicry primarily map to bad-faith rendering (sub-primitive §2.3); Müllerian primarily maps to memetic warfare (§2.2) when coordinated; aggressive mimicry primarily maps to extractive wrapper-overlap (§2.6) in its acquisition phase. The mappings are not 1:1; the ecosystem hosts mixed strategies.

### §2.2 Hijackers as ecological parasites (Pav's "hijacers")

Parasitism is the more aggressive parallel strategy. The hijacker doesn't just exploit partial information — it actively captures another organism's infrastructure for its own ends.

**Brood parasitism.** An organism places its offspring in another's nest, exploiting the host's caregiving behavior. Cuckoos, cowbirds. Structurally: placing one's own canon-payload inside another's already-trusted distribution channel. *Substrate signature:* the host substrate's existing care-discipline becomes the attack vector — the more the substrate values the channel's trust, the more it propagates the parasitic payload.

**Lateral gene transfer.** Bacteria swap genes across lineages, including via phage vectors that exploit cellular machinery. Structurally: hijacker captures an institution's compiled-canon (legal authority, brand legitimacy, embedded credentials) and re-uses it for purposes orthogonal to original canon-formation. *Substrate signature:* the hijacked institution's external appearance is preserved while internal canon is silently modified. Maps to canon hijacking (candidate §2.1).

**Vector parasitism.** Use of another's infrastructure (transport, distribution, communication) to spread. Maps to memetic warfare's amplification dynamics and L0 mediator capture.

The ecological vocabulary is sharper than the candidate doc's "broken symmetry of substrate dynamics" because it names the strategic moves the adversary makes — not just that the result is asymmetric. A hijacker doesn't accidentally end up asymmetric; it pursues a specific evolved strategy.

### §2.3 Coevolutionary arms race (Red Queen dynamics)

The most important transfer. From Van Valen 1973: in coevolving systems, organisms must continuously evolve just to maintain relative fitness. The substrate's selection pressure produces the adversary; the adversary produces selection pressure on the substrate's discriminative capacity; the substrate evolves better discrimination; the adversary evolves better mimicry. The system never reaches equilibrium because the equilibrium itself is the contested ground.

**Substrate-canonical example:** Google search ranking ↔ SEO industry. Google publishes ranking signals → SEO industry optimizes to those signals → results become spammy → Google updates ranking criteria → SEO industry adapts to new criteria. This loop has run continuously since circa 1998. Each Google algorithm update is a substrate-selection-pressure event; each SEO adaptation is an evolved-counter-response. No equilibrium reached after 25+ years.

**Other canonical examples:** spam filters ↔ spammers (Paul Graham's 2002 "A Plan for Spam" was first formalization of the dynamic at email substrate); detection models ↔ adversarial examples in ML; CAPTCHAs ↔ CAPTCHA-solving services and bots; content moderation ↔ rule-evasion patterns; anti-virus ↔ malware obfuscation.

**Why this is load-bearing for the candidate:** the framework's existing cont 13 A⁻/A⁺ coupled discipline assumes A⁻ tightens against existing A⁺ noise distribution. Coevolution says the noise distribution adapts to A⁻ — the A⁻ discipline that worked yesterday is by-construction less effective today because adversarial A⁺ has fitted to it. The framework needs to name this evolved adaptation; it's not just "noise gets through sometimes" but "noise actively evolves to bypass the specific A⁻ discipline you're using."

This sharpens [Reading 07's BES Theorem 4.4 entropy-shell escape](../readings/2026-05-31_bes_bidirectional_evolutionary_search.md) at adversarial substrate: not only does the framework need recombination operators to escape auto-regressive entropy shells, but the adversarial population is actively recombining its own attacks to escape the defender's entropy shells. Bidirectional coevolution at attack-defense substrate.

### §2.4 Niche construction

Organisms modify their environment, changing the selection pressure their descendants and other species face. Beavers build dams. Earthworms aerate soil. The framework's analog: adversarial actors don't just adapt to substrate selection — they reshape the substrate's selection criteria to favor their fitness.

**Internet examples:** SEO industry's collective lobbying for "white-hat" practices defines what Google can legitimately punish without losing market share. Influence operations' co-evolution with platform content policies shape what speech-acts are permissible. AI-content generators' deployment changes what humans expect content to look like, which shifts the discriminative baseline for "natural-seeming" content.

**Structural import:** niche construction is what makes adversarial substrate dynamics not just a flow but a **substrate-modifying** force. The substrate that defenders are protecting today is not the substrate they were protecting yesterday — partly because the adversaries modified it. This is structurally adjacent to cont 19's everything-aggregator/moving-target canon — the target moves not just because the framework expands but because adversaries reshape the substrate.

### §2.5 Ecosystem failure modes (different from individual failure)

Individual sub-primitive failures (one regulatory capture event, one disinformation campaign, one botnet) are different in kind from ecosystem-level failures. Four canonical ecosystem failure modes transfer:

**Monoculture collapse.** When selection pressure rewards one fitness criterion overwhelmingly, all evolved actors converge on it; novelty drops; the system becomes fragile to any disturbance that escapes the single criterion. Substrate-canonical: when all content optimizes for engagement metrics, the substrate's signal-to-noise collapses to engagement-bait. The substrate retains "function" (content keeps being produced) but loses substrate-properties (truth-tracking, diversity, signal).

**Trophic collapse.** When extractors exceed substrate's regenerative capacity, the substrate degrades faster than it regenerates. Eventual collapse of substrate; extractors move on; substrate-participants left with collapsed substrate. Substrate-canonical: any platform where attention-extraction exceeds creator-renewal eventually loses its creators. Maps to Cory Doctorow's enshittification thesis at structural level.

**Invasive species without native predators.** New substrate-introduced actor faces no evolved counter-strategies; population explodes unchecked until substrate is overwhelmed. Substrate-canonical: AI-generated content at scale circa 2023-2025 — generation capabilities outpaced detection capabilities by structural lag (generation models train on detection patterns; detection models can only train on existing generated content; structural asymmetry favors generation). This is happening LIVE on the internet substrate as of cont 31's drafting.

**Keystone species capture.** A small-population actor whose role is structurally load-bearing; removal cascades through ecosystem. Substrate-canonical: a small number of L0-mediator institutions (Google, Meta, Apple, OpenAI, Anthropic) whose decisions cascade through the internet ecosystem. Capture or compromise of any one would cascade. The keystone-species framing makes the L0-mediator-capture sub-primitive (§2.4) more empirically tractable — you can identify ecosystem-level keystones by removal-test reasoning.

## §4 Internet as ground zero — why this substrate first

Pav's "internet is ground zero" steer is empirically right for four structural reasons.

**§4.1 Latent space is the densest and most measurable there.** Internet substrate runs on explicit latent-space mechanisms: embedding manifolds (word2vec onward), recommender systems (collaborative-filtering latent factors), search ranking (PageRank vector + relevance-vector dot products), LLM-mediated agent-substrate (transformer hidden states as latent canon-protocol). This makes the candidate's adversarial dynamics empirically tractable in a way that, say, abusive-relationship dynamics at intimate substrate are not. You can measure embedding-similarity attacks; you can audit recommender-system manipulation; you can detect Müllerian-mimic clusters statistically.

This sharpens [cont 17 §3 latent-space Tier 3 speculation](17.md). Cont 17 held latent-substrate-as-fundamental as structurally coherent but unfalsifiable. The internet-substrate refinement is narrower and more empirically tractable: latent space is empirically load-bearing AT the internet substrate (search, recommendation, LLM, embedding). Whether it's fundamental everywhere is the unfalsifiable cont 17 question; whether it's the operative substrate for adversarial dynamics on the internet is empirically checkable.

**§4.2 The L0 mediators are explicit and named.** Reading 08's L0-mediator-as-Tier-2-algorithmically-demonstrated framework is most directly applicable on the internet — Google's PageRank, Meta's EdgeRank, TikTok's For-You algorithm, OpenAI's RLHF reward model, Anthropic's Constitutional-AI process — each is an algorithmically-instantiated L0 mediator for its substrate. Each is also a documented keystone species per §2.5. Each has documented capture-attempts, coevolution-with-adversaries, and emergent ecosystem-level dynamics.

**§4.3 The coevolution arms races are documented and dated.** Unlike many substrates where adversarial dynamics are inferred from outcomes, the internet substrate has dated documentation of arms-race iterations:
- 1995-2025+: Google ranking algorithm updates (Florida 2003, Panda 2011, Penguin 2012, BERT 2019, MUM 2021, generative-AI-content waves 2023-2025) ↔ SEO industry adaptations
- 2002+: spam filtering (Bayesian, then ML, then transformer-based) ↔ spam-adversarial-text generation (poisoning, prompt-injection, AI-generated spam)
- 2014+: deep-learning adversarial examples (Goodfellow et al. 2014) ↔ adversarial training and certified defenses
- 2016+: Russian troll-farm / IRA operations ↔ platform integrity teams ↔ next-generation influence-operation infrastructure
- 2019+: deepfakes ↔ deepfake detection (with structural detection-lag becoming more pronounced 2023-2025)
- 2023+: LLM prompt injection / jailbreaking ↔ red-teaming and safety training
- 2023+: AI-generated content saturation ↔ proposed watermarking / provenance standards (C2PA etc.)

Each is a named coevolution dynamic with dated milestones. This makes Bar A worked-example development tractable: pick one arms race, document the coevolution structurally, surface ecosystem-level features (selection pressure, niche construction, monoculture collapse risk, etc.) that the existing per-event analysis misses.

**§4.4 Ecosystem failure modes are currently visible.** Three of the four §2.5 failure modes are arguably running on the internet substrate live as of 2026:
- Monoculture collapse via engagement-optimization (signal-to-noise widely discussed; "dead internet theory" is the popular framing; structural framing: substrate's selection criteria converged to single criterion → diversity loss → fragility)
- Invasive-species explosion via AI-generated content (generation capability vastly exceeds detection capability; substrate is being overwhelmed; the structural-asymmetry argument in §2.5 explains why this isn't a transient problem)
- Trophic collapse risk in attention-extraction platforms (enshittification thesis at structural level)

This is the candidate's first empirically-visible substrate where the ecosystem framing makes predictions the existing framework didn't.

## §6 What ecosystem framing makes more legible than the per-sub-primitive view

Six concrete observations the framework couldn't make from the candidate doc alone:

**§6.1 Attacks are not isolated.** A single phishing campaign is not just §2.6 asymmetric extraction; it's a Batesian mimic occupying a niche the substrate's selection criteria created. Patching the campaign doesn't patch the niche. New phishing fills the niche because the niche persists.

**§6.2 Defenses produce next-generation attacks.** Spam filtering became more sophisticated; spam became AI-generated. CAPTCHA defeated naive bots; bot-services emerged. Content moderation expanded; rule-evasion patterns evolved. This is canonical Red Queen. The candidate doc's existing primitives don't predict this; the ecosystem framing makes it the expected behavior.

**§6.3 Adversarial populations occupy multiple niches simultaneously.** A nation-state-actor running an influence operation simultaneously runs memetic warfare (§2.2 Müllerian mimicry across coordinated accounts), bad-faith rendering (§2.3 personas presenting genuine-citizen face), aggressive mimicry (§2.6 targeted scams of high-value individuals), and potentially L0 mediator capture (§2.4 via platform-policy lobbying or insider compromise). The candidate doc lists six sub-primitives separately; the ecosystem framing predicts they co-occur because the same actor benefits from filling multiple niches.

**§6.4 Substrate's discriminative capacity creates next mimicry frontier.** When LLM-detection models reach 90% accuracy at distinguishing human-from-AI text, AI-generation models train against detection-model signal and reach evade-capability. This is structural, not avoidable through better defense — it's the equilibrium-dynamics of an arms race where attacker has access to defender's signal. The candidate doc's existing primitives don't say this is structural; the ecosystem framing predicts it.

**§6.5 Substrate health is ecosystem-level, not patch-level.** "Is the internet substrate healthy?" is not answerable by counting individual attacks. It's answerable by ecosystem-level metrics: diversity, signal-to-noise, niche-occupancy distribution, novel-actor emergence rate, substrate-renewal rate vs extraction rate. The candidate doc has wrapper-overlap-symmetry as the per-encounter health metric; the ecosystem framing provides the system-level health metric the candidate was implicitly groping toward.

**§6.6 Adversarial-population control requires reshaping selection criteria, not patching mimics.** Standard substrate-defender intuition: detect and remove bad actors. Ecosystem intuition: the substrate's selection criteria created the niche; until the selection criteria change, the niche persists and is occupied by whoever evolves to fit. Pat answers: stop ranking on engagement (substrate-selection change); add provenance to L0 mediator's compile-loop (substrate-discriminative-capacity change); change the substrate's reproductive incentives (substrate-renewal change). These are upstream of any specific mimic detection. The framework gets a vocabulary for upstream-substrate-design as a defense layer that didn't exist in the per-sub-primitive view.

## §7 The internet-ground-zero worked example sketch — search ranking ecosystem

Pav's steer named internet as ground zero. The candidate's Bar A worked-example targets in §8 list six options ranked by tractability. The ecosystem framing makes the SEARCH RANKING ECOSYSTEM as a whole the most-tractable worked example (more tractable than any individual sub-primitive case) because it has 25+ years of documented coevolution, multiple identified niches, named selection pressure events, and currently-visible ecosystem failure mode.

This is a sketch, not a finished Bar A worked example. The finished work-up would require dedicated session time and is a candidate carry-forward.

**§7.1 The substrate.** Internet search results, with Google's PageRank and successor algorithms as the L0 mediator. Substrate-actors: content publishers (sites), end-users (searchers), advertisers (paying for distribution), substrate-mediator (Google).

**§7.2 The selection pressure.** Google's ranking criteria function as substrate selection criteria. Sites that rank well survive; sites that don't get progressively less traffic; eventually unranked sites become non-viable. The selection pressure is structural and continuous.

**§7.3 The niches.** Multiple adversarial niches occupied simultaneously:
- **White-hat SEO niche** — operators optimizing within published-ranking-criteria (substrate-actors honestly competing for ranking)
- **Grey-hat SEO niche** — operators using techniques Google hasn't yet declared adversarial but is structurally extracting value (Batesian mimicry of high-authority signals)
- **Black-hat SEO niche** — operators using declared-adversarial techniques (keyword stuffing, link farms, cloaking)
- **Content-farm niche** — high-volume low-quality A⁺ production (memetic warfare against ranking-A⁻)
- **AI-content niche** (post-2022) — invasive species with no native predators; generation capability vastly exceeds Google's structural detection capability for at-scale AI content
- **Brand-hijacking niche** — adversarial domains mimicking trusted brands for click-capture (aggressive mimicry)
- **Negative-SEO niche** — adversarial actors attacking rivals' rankings (Müllerian-adjacent inverse: coordinated negative-signal injection)

**§7.4 The coevolution.** Each Google algorithm update is a selection-pressure event; each SEO industry adaptation is an evolved response. 25+ years of documented iteration. Each Google update permanently modifies the niche distribution; some niches collapse, new niches open. The substrate-selection landscape is itself constantly being modified by both Google (top-down) and SEO industry (bottom-up niche construction).

**§7.5 The ecosystem failure mode currently visible.** Post-2022, AI-generated content is structurally outcompeting human-curated content in many query-spaces. Detection lag is structural (generation models train against detection models; detection models can only train on existing AI content). The substrate's signal-to-noise is degrading; users observe "search has gotten worse" widely; substrate's viability for original purpose (find authoritative information) is empirically degrading. This is invasive-species-without-native-predators ecosystem failure mode actively running.

**§7.6 What the ecosystem framing predicts the per-sub-primitive view doesn't.** Without ecosystem framing, the dominant response is "Google should detect-and-demote AI content." Ecosystem framing predicts: (a) detection-and-demotion creates selection pressure that produces AI-content evolution toward detection-evasion; (b) detection-vs-evasion arms race favors evasion structurally because evolution rate of AI generation models exceeds evolution rate of detection models; (c) the ecosystem-level solution requires reshaping selection criteria (e.g., signal provenance, retrieval-from-attested-sources, computation-cost as ranking signal) not improving detection. This is a different family of interventions than the per-sub-primitive view would identify.

**§7.7 What this worked example sketch demonstrates for Bar A.** The ecosystem framing of the search-ranking case makes predictions and identifies intervention-classes that the existing neutral framework didn't surface. Per the candidate doc's §5 Bar A criteria: "[if predictions / analyses] are structurally novel and survive cont 27 §2 tier-tagging, Bar A is met." This sketch is not yet a finished worked example, but it points in a direction that could reach Bar A with dedicated session time. Pav's call on whether to develop it fully or pick a different §8 worked-example target.
