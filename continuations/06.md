## 2. The agnostic unit (AU) defined

### 2.1 Core definition

```text
AU(O, t) = P × A matrix at time t for observer O

where:
  P = set of perceivable signals available to observer O
  A = set of actions available to observer O
  cell(p, a) = weighted probability that signal p induces action a
```

The unit's "size" is some norm of the matrix — trace, determinant, spectral radius, or a learned aggregate. The choice of norm itself becomes a framework parameter and may be observer-class-dependent.

### 2.2 Why "perceivable action space" is the right anchor

The unit is **observer-relative**, not absolute. This preserves the framework's central commitment that there is no external vantage. Other candidate anchors (energy, information, complexity) all imply a privileged observer who can measure them absolutely; perceivable action space is intrinsically tied to *which observer*.

This connects to a 100-year-old prior-art concept: Jakob von Uexküll's **umwelt** (1909) — the perceptual world of an organism. A tick's umwelt is dominated by butyric acid and skin temperature. A human's includes color vision, language, abstract concepts. An LLM's includes token-distributions over a vocabulary. An institution's includes financial signals and legal pressures. Each umwelt is a completely different *shape*; the *structural concept* is universal.

The agnostic unit is essentially a generalized umwelt, operationalized as a measurable quantity with weighted dimensions. Uexküll gave the concept; the framework gives it the math.

### 2.3 Observer-class shapes

| Observer class | P shape | A shape | Density |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell | Small (chemical gradients, mechanical stimuli) | Small (motility, gene expression) | Sparse, few cells with high weights |
| Person | Medium (rich sensorium, language, social cues) | Medium (speech, action, tool-use) | Variable; dense in habituated regions, sparse elsewhere |
| LLM | Token contexts up to context window | Next-token distributions | Dense; rotating weights per checkpoint |
| Institution | Large (financial, legal, public, internal signals) | Large (policy, hiring, marketing, lobbying) | Mostly aggregated from member AUs |
| Civilization | Vast P | Vast A | Most cells near zero; a few highly active |

Same matrix *type*; different parameter values. That is the fractal claim made formal.

## 3. Fractal-matrix structure

### 3.1 Nesting

An observer's AU can contain other AUs as sub-matrices. The block structure represents the containment relation:

```text
Institution_AU =
  | sub-matrices of member AUs |
  | aggregation operators that combine them |
  | institution-specific dimensions not present in any member AU |
```

This is structurally a hypergraph of matrices with containment edges.

### 3.2 Overlap

Two observers can share rows (perceivable signals both can detect) or columns (actions both can take). Their AUs overlap in those rows/columns. The intersection is a sub-matrix; the union is a larger matrix.

```text
overlap(O1, O2) = AU(O1) ∩ AU(O2)
  - shared signals: rows in both P sets
  - shared actions: columns in both A sets
  - shared weights: cells where (p, a) is in both
```

Symbiosis becomes formally measurable: a symbiotic merge is admissible when the merged AU norm exceeds the sum of individual norms minus friction.

### 3.3 Why "fractal" is the right word

Self-similar structural template at multiple scales, without being identical at multiple scales. A cell's AU and a civilization's AU have the same *structural type* (P × A matrix with weighted cells) but completely different *content*. The framework's universal claim isn't about specific values; it's about the recurring shape.

This is the technical sense of fractal — Mandelbrot's self-similarity — not the colloquial sense of "looks like itself zoomed in."

## 6. Caveats

### 6.1 The AU represents; it does not constitute

The agnostic unit is a carrier-artifact representation of an observer at a level of abstraction. It is **not** the observer itself. The framework's own vocabulary warns here: every successful canon eventually becomes a stale moat. The temptation, once you have a unit and a matrix, is to claim the matrix *is* the observer rather than that it *describes* the observer for operational purposes.

The discipline: the AU is useful, measurable, and partial. The observer always exceeds its representation. Saying this explicitly is the inoculation against overreach.

### 6.2 The matrix shape is one of many

A P × A matrix is one structural choice. Others are possible:

- **Tensor:** add dimensions for context, time, internal state, energy
- **Category:** observers as objects, action-mappings as morphisms (Spivak / Goguen direction)
- **Hypergraph:** explicit representation of nested + overlapping structure with no pretense of being a single matrix
- **Sheaf:** observer as local section over a base space, with gluing conditions across overlaps (Goguen)

The P × A matrix is the simplest form that captures the move. Production implementations will likely use richer structures. The framework's universal claim should be agnostic about which mathematical form wins — what matters is that *some* such form exists and is observer-relative.

### 6.3 Weights are not absolute either

Per the framework's own logic, the *measurement of weights* is itself an action by some observer. There is no observer-free weight assignment. This is recursive: an outside-world reading by agent X produces weighted AU matrices that are themselves X's interpretation of the targets. This is fine — the framework already accepts that all readings are observer-relative. But it should be said explicitly in the math.
