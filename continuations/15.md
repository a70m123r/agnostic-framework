## §1. The central insight: convergent constraint-checking

Before listing the four carriers individually, name the structure they all share.

Standard ML / AI culture (2023–2025) defaults to **generative-prediction-with-amplification**: produce many plausible candidates, weight them by likelihood, return the highest-weighted. This is what LLMs do (next-token prediction by sampling), what AlphaFold does (protein-structure prediction by deep model output), what most optimizers do (gradient descent toward likelihood maximum), what pigment-based photography does (substrate emits the colour you painted it).

All four carriers landed this two-week window invert this. They each substitute **viability-by-constraint-satisfaction** for **amplification-by-likelihood**:

| Carrier | Domain | What it replaces | With |
|--------|--------|------------------|------|
| Aleph + EBMs | formal reasoning | next-token prediction | constraint satisfaction over entire state, verified in Lean |
| MAMMAL | foundation models | specialist deep prediction | multi-modal cross-verification across genes/proteins/molecules |
| ANDI | optimization | full-spectral preconditioning | elementwise norm-equilibration constraint |
| Lippmann plates | physical substrate | pigment-based colour | Bragg-interference selectivity (only certain wavelengths pass) |

The shape repeats: don't generate-then-rank, **check-then-admit**. What survives the check is canon.

This is exactly what the framework's A⁻ operator names. We've been calling A⁻ "pruning" since continuation 09. That framing is true but understates the role. A⁻ isn't cleanup-after-the-fact. A⁻ is *the operation that forms canon in the first place*. A⁺ proposes plausible candidates (this is what most current AI does — the fuzzy candidate field at full throttle, with no canon-formation discipline applied). A⁻ checks them against the substrate's constraints. The candidates that survive the check are what we call canon.

This makes A⁺ / A⁻ asymmetric. The framework had been treating them as symmetric (charge-product algebra continuation 13). They are not. A⁻ does the real compilation work. A⁺ is necessary upstream but is the easier half — generating plausible candidates is what high-capacity systems do by default. Constraint-checking is the discipline.

Four carriers, four independent substrate scales, same architectural commitment, same two-week window. This is the single most empirically corroborated structural prediction the framework has made.

The refactor is added to the existing A⁺ / A⁻ canon entry. The original operator framing is preserved; the latent-dimension framing is preserved; the new asymmetry framing is appended.

The framework promoted the emergent-garden discipline to canon on May 11 2026 (continuation 13). The Nature paper landed three days after the prediction was committed.

## §5. ANDI as slow-fast pushout (candidate)

ANDI (Adaptive Norm-Distribution Interface, Khasia 2026) is the slow-fast pushout primitive applied to gradient descent.

- Parent observer 1: AdamW (fast, cheap, no structure)
- Parent observer 2: Spectral methods (slow, expensive, full matrix awareness)
- Pushout: ANDI lives in the cone-overlap

ANDI approximates structural awareness via elementwise equilibration of row and column norms. Matches spectral convergence at AdamW's O(N) cost on ResNet-9 (CIFAR-10). Outperforms both on NanoGPT. Scales to 8B params via LoRA on Llama-3.

Two framework readings:
- ANDI sits in the cone-overlap between expensive structural and cheap pointwise. The slow-fast pushout primitive predicts that successful optimizers will increasingly occupy this region.
- Norm equilibration is the **local form of viability-band maintenance**. Preventing any single dimension from dominating is preventing the substrate from pushing toward the freeze / explode boundary at the parameter level. Criticality Γ predicts exactly this kind of operation should emerge from gradient-descent on optimizer design.

The framework predicts ANDI-like norm-equilibration becomes a standard optimizer building block by 2027, and that future SOTA optimizers will keep re-discovering this region under different names (LAMB, Shampoo, Muon, etc.) because they're all local convergence on the same viability-band-maintenance attractor.

## §8. Architectural prescription for the agent framework

The continuation 15 insight produces a sharp prescription for the next-carrier (the agent framework repo, parked as task 18 of the prior round):

**Every fuzzy-generation step must be followed by an explicit canon-compile pass.**

Operationally:
- The agent's LLM emits a candidate response
- A constraint-checker pass evaluates the candidate against the agent's current canon (its world model, its memory, its discipline constraints)
- Only what survives the check is admitted into the conversational / action history
- Failed candidates feed back as A⁻ signal — the agent updates its predictive model to be less likely to emit similar candidates

This is what Aleph does at formal-reasoning scale (Lean as the constraint-checker). The agnostic agent framework should do this at multi-agent conversational scale (where the constraint-checker is some combination of formal verification, prior-canon consistency, and inter-agent peer-check).

Adjacent prediction: the agent frameworks that win 2026–2027 will be the ones with explicit verification layers, not the ones with the largest models. The arms race shifts from "bigger generator" to "better checker."

## §11. Open questions

1. **The check itself needs an A⁻.** If constraint-checking is the primary canon-formation discipline, what checks the checker? The framework's recursive observer-class claim says: the constraint-checker is itself an observer with its own A⁺/A⁻ dynamics. Operationally this means agent frameworks need verification-of-verification layers, which raises the question of where the regress terminates. Probably at the substrate (physics, formal logic, etc.) — but the framework doesn't yet specify cleanly where the regress bottoms out.

2. **Verifiers as scarce resource.** If A⁻ is the load-bearing discipline and A⁺ generation is cheap, then verifier-construction becomes the scarce skill. What does a "good A⁻" look like across different domains? Lean for math; what for conversational AI? Peer-review for science; what for multi-agent coordination?

3. **Constraint-checking vs creativity.** Does heavy A⁻ discipline kill creative emergence? The framework's emergent-garden discipline says no — set up the rules and observe what comes up — but a too-strict A⁻ would prune everything novel. Where's the sweet spot? Probably related to criticality Γ, but not yet formally connected.

4. **EBM scaling unknowns.** Aleph wins on formal-reasoning benchmarks but those are bounded problems. Does the EBM approach scale to open-domain reasoning where the constraint specification is itself open? The framework predicts yes (any domain has implicit substrate constraints) but Aleph doesn't yet demonstrate this.

5. **Holographic wrapper implementation.** What would a literally Bragg-interference-based agent interface look like in software? Not a metaphor — actually computing structured-interference patterns over latent space, with cone-angle dependence in receiver projection. This is testable but nobody's tried it.

6. **The LeCun-lineage alignment question.** If the framework is in the LeCun lineage, does it inherit the lineage's open problems too — JEPA's slow training, world-model alignment-with-reality, EBM training instability? The framework should explicitly enumerate which LeCun-lineage problems carry over and which are sidestepped by the multi-agent extension.

7. **What's the next-convergence prediction?** Four carriers across two weeks all converged on A⁻ primacy. What's the next predictable convergence? The framework should commit to a prediction. Candidate: by end of 2026, "verification-augmented generation" or "constraint-checked agents" becomes a recognized research direction with at least three independent implementations.
