Dialogue · entry 01 · the framework in conversation

A historian, an AI scientist, and a philosopher in one room. The agnostic framework is not the topic — it is the bridge that lets them talk to each other instead of past each other. Below is how the conversation opens.

HISTORIAN
Every major civilizational transition in the past five thousand years has the same shape. A new substrate enables a new kind of observer; the new kind doesn't replace the old but absorbs it as constraint. Writing didn't replace oral memory; it embedded oral memory inside literate civilization. The question I keep returning to is whether what we're watching with AI is a transition of that scale, or merely a technology shift inside the existing one.
SCIENTIST
I'd push back on "merely a technology shift" before we even start. In the lab I'm watching something genuinely new. Whether or not it crosses some philosophical threshold for "real cognition," it's functionally novel. The bracket — transition versus technology — feels false to me.
PHILOSOPHER
I think you're both right and you're talking past each other. You don't share a vocabulary for "what is a substrate" or "what is an observer" — so you can't have the same conversation. A framework I've been reading is designed for exactly this situation. It's a vocabulary for how new things emerge from overlap of old things, at any scale, without committing to the ontology.
HISTORIAN
Go on.

03Primitives

The framework's vocabulary, scored honestly. Each primitive carries its current status: canon (load-bearing, survived many tests), candidate (proposed, plausible, not yet validated), or speculative (held in suspension, openly uncertain). Filter to see one category.

Vocabulary primer · cont 22

The framework keeps canon as its central word and differentiates with modifiers. Two co-primary forms; four classes that stack; two cross-cutting parameterizations.

Real canon and latent canon — the two co-primary forms. Real canon is the physical/reality side: gravity, time, cause-and-effect, information transport, the math substrate the universe runs on. Latent canon is the in-the-head side: embeddings, memory, the field of meaning a mind operates within. Neither reduces to the other; they are polar opposites joined by inverse-coherence.

Four canon classes form a stack. Kernel canon is the raw substrate at the deepest layer — what an observer finds rather than makes. Compile it (via the framework's compile loop: A⁺/A⁻, carriers, friction band) and you get compiled canon — the institutional/protocol level, the patterns an observer-class lives by. Embody compiled canon in a specific carrier and you get a canon artefact — a particular constitution, a particular ritual, a particular model file. Run that artefact and the lived operation is function canon.

Two cross-cutting parameterizations apply at every layer. Real vs latent — every layer has a real face and a latent face. Top / centre / sub — every layer has a three-position vertical scope. Centre canon is the observer's own (the wrapper's prism refraction of the whole — what was originally called global canon). Sub canon is what's below the observer (subconscious processing for human; aggregated outputs of lower-ring sub-observers composing the harness). Top canon is what's above the observer (the broader context the observer is embedded in but cannot fully render — including the substrate the higher-ring observer-class treats as kernel). Sub and top are polar opposites; centre canon is where the wrapper's render mediates between them.

All of it is observer-relative. What counts as kernel vs compiled vs artefact vs function depends on which observer-class is reading. Drill down procedurally and a function canon resolves into the canon artefact a lower ring produced; that artefact resolves into the compiled canon of the ring below; and so on, bottoming at the kernel canon a given observer-class can no longer drill through. 08Contribute

The framework's discipline says it must earn its keep in your life or get pruned. If you want to participate, here is what useful contribution looks like.

Submit a reading

Pick a real situation. Apply the framework's primitives to it. Make at least one prediction the reading commits to. Send it in. If it parses cleanly and the prediction has falsifiable shape, it goes in §03 with your name on it.

Flag where it breaks

If the framework fails to translate to something in your life — your work, your field, your experience — that is a data point worth more than a thousand confirmations. Tell me where the vocabulary stops being useful and what you tried.

Argue with a primitive

Take a canon item from §02 and try to break it. If your argument holds, the primitive moves to candidate or gets pruned, and your counter-evidence gets preserved as the reason. The framework is supposed to be malleable; demonstrate it.

Run an experiment

The research edition lists two falsifiable side-experiments (SDPO+ on SOTOPIA; protein-LM on raw network packets). If you have compute, time, or relevant expertise — run one. Results go up regardless of which direction they point.

Annotate any paragraph

Click the floating tab at the right edge of any page, sign in (free), and you can highlight any sentence or paragraph and leave a comment, a counter-reading, a source, or a correction attached to that exact spot. Annotations are public by default. The framework's A⁻ checking is supposed to be visible; this is the carrier that makes it visible to anyone reading.

Slow-fast pushout in real time. Neither voice would produce v6 alone. Continuation 16 names this pattern — carrier before canon — as methodology.

Six iterations. That's the framework's symbiosis-as-pushout primitive operating in the act of making the artifact that describes it. The carrier compiled before its canon entry.
