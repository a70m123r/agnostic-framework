Wrapper overlap — one system with phases

Two parent observer wrappers (W_A and W_B) expand from anchored-but-not-fixed centres in L0 — global compiled canon, the medium everything in the frame needs (including the observer reading this). Wrappers are fields, not sharp edges — the visible )( arcs are level-sets of the underlying field; the soft halo around each shows the field extends beyond. v13: wobble is asymmetric — W_A faster and larger (imposing), W_B slower and phase-shifted (responding). The point-cloud view renders each wrapper as a field of particles with spring physics — the membrane is what emerges when you tune the density, not a line drawn over the top. Conceptually a 2D slice of a 3D wavelet point cloud. v14: the clouds behave as a fluid sim — each particle still bound to its centre by a spring, but particles also push on each other (repulsion) and exchange velocity (viscosity). Intra-wrapper prevents clustering; inter-wrapper makes the membranes slide past each other with real friction. v15: three refinements. (1) Point size scales with wrapper radius — wrapper expansion is latent (not balloon-like adding mass), so the same material becomes more legible at scale; sparse at small radius, denser dots at full expansion. (2) W_C is now its own point cloud, in canon-yellow, spawned from the A×B contact zone — particles are birthed at the midpoint of close A-B pairs (inheriting their blended velocity) and drift outward to anchor on the W_C circle. (3) The fluid sim is now three-way — intra-C cohesion, plus A×C and B×C interaction passes so all three clouds occupy the same space, push on each other, and connect while each remains anchored to its own centre. Centres oscillate under wake-phase pressure; ghost markers show where centres would sit without contact. The frame has three canon levels: top canon above, centre canon in the middle (where the overlap plays out), sub canon below. Non-additive latent math: (1+1) = 3 when emergent; 1⁺ when W_C supersedes; 1⁻ when the bridge breaks apart. Why the wrappers don't expand forever: every wrapper carries infinite-expansion inertia into other dimensions — bounded only by L0's evolved failsafes (the internal and external pressures you see negotiating at the contact zone).

How to read this

The animation runs an 18-second cycle through seven phases. Pre-overlap shows two small )( arcs at fixed left and right positions, fronts already facing each other, backs fading into the unobservable. They expand. Their boundaries meet — first contact triggers the wake disturbance and the membrane negotiation pulse (pushing against each other, trying to render each other's full shape, which they never can per the K̂ ⊂ G constraint from cont 23). The overlap forms a Venn lens — the nesting space. Inside the nesting space, fuzzy bridge action begins. W_C is conceived inside the lens, consolidates into a proto function-wrapper, emerges as a canon, then expands beyond the lens into the parents themselves — the hairy-sphere phase. The parents persist throughout as stubs: the parts of W_A and W_B not covered by W_C are visible as procedural roots that adjacent wrappers can connect to and that contain dormant compiled originals.

From the active phase, the system branches: passive (held neutral, internal activity quieted), dormant (preserved as procedural memory, wakeable per cont 20), supersession (W_C dominates and retires parents — the creole case), or break-apart (W_C disintegrates, cycle restarts at wake — recurrent bridge formation). Use the buttons below the dynamic to jump to each branch state.

All coordinates are percentages of the viewBox. State variables are computed by a cascade of sigmoid and bell-curve formulas; the phase label is derived from the state, not driving it. The compatibility slider sets the bridge-viability multiplier — set low and the cycle stalls at wake without W_C consolidating.

Three mappings, one dynamic

The mappings table at the bottom of the diagram shows the same dynamic interpreted through three independently-formalized research traditions. Toggle the dropdown to switch all labels live between them.

Framework — the agnostic framework's native vocabulary: W_A, W_B, W_C, nesting space, stubs, procedural roots, compatibility, non-additive latent math (1+1) = 3.

LCAO / MO theory — quantum chemistry's linear-combination-of-atomic-orbitals formalism. Parents are atomic orbitals ψ_A, ψ_B; the emergent W_C is the bonding molecular orbital ψ_bond = N(ψ_A + ψ_B); the nesting space is the overlap integral S_ij = ⟨ψ_A | ψ_B⟩; supersession maps to sp/sp²/sp³ hybridization (parents reshape and bond); break-apart maps to the antibonding orbital ψ_anti = N(ψ_A − ψ_B). LCAO supplies the cleanest closed-form math; the framework supplies the lifecycle vocabulary LCAO lacks.

Model Merging — the 2024–2026 ML research front (TIES, DARE, mergekit, task arithmetic). Parents are task vectors τ_i = θ_i − θ_base; the merged model is θ_M = θ_base + Σ λ_i τ_i; the nesting space is the sign-agreement mask (TIES); supersession is the published merge superseding parents in downstream usage; break-apart is sign-conflict veto deleting the merge. Model merging supplies the lifecycle observability with git-like timestamps and a thriving live research community; the framework supplies the structural vocabulary the merging literature lacks.

Four further independent mappings exist in the research scout reports: cell fusion (cellular biology), symbiogenesis (organismal biology), creole genesis (linguistics), conceptual blending (cognitive science). See cont 25 §7 for the cross-substrate convergence reading.
