§01What is Michotte's Launching paradigm

Belgian psychologist Albert Michotte's The Perception of Causality (1946/63) opened the modern empirical study of causality perception. In his paradigm, a square moves toward a stationary square. The moving square reaches the stationary one. Under specific spatiotemporal conditions — chiefly spatial contact and a temporal gap below ~100ms between contact and the second square's motion onset — observers do not just see two motions. They perceive direct CAUSATION. One event launches another. Violate the conditions (large gap, no contact, B moves in the wrong direction) and the unity dissolves into two perceptually distinct events.

Michotte's threshold is robust across replications. The temporal binding window has been refined since (Wallace & Stevenson 2014, Eagleman and successors) but the basic finding is canonical cognitive science: causality is partly a perceptual category, with measurable thresholds, not a pure cognitive inference.

§02What this extension adds beyond classical Michotte

Classical Michotte varies only the physical parameters: gap timing, spatial contact, direction. The observer is treated as a passive registrar. This extension treats the observer as a parameterizable observer-class per the agnostic framework. Two new parameters change what gets perceived even when physical parameters are held fixed:

ParameterWhat it doesFramework primitive it maps to
Mechanical Delay
0–800ms slider		 The classical Michotte parameter. Sets the actual time between A's contact with B and B's onset of motion. Below ~100ms → classical launching territory. Above → classical disconnection territory. Substrate-level temporal parameter. The thing the framework's claims about the observer are claims about.
Attention Sampling
10–100% slider		 Determines how frequently the observer samples events. At 100% → continuous sampling (effectively no gaps in attention). At 10% → infrequent samples; events between samples are not perceived. Cont 26 §4 — perceptual rate as a sense. Promoted to Tier 1 canon. Different observers (different attention rates) get different fidelity reads of the same physical event.
Sensory Bridging
checkbox		 Toggles cross-modal integration. When on, the observer's temporal integration window widens (~300ms vs ~100ms visual-only). The empirical claim being tested: cross-modal binding can preserve causality perception at delays where pure visual binding fails. Cont 14 holographic-wrapper + cont 16 membrane-as-perceptual-surface. The membrane samples across modalities; bridging composes the samples into a unified percept.
Speed
0.25–2x slider		 Controls the simulation's compilation rate. At slow speeds you see fine-grained dynamics; at fast speeds you see only the gross outcome. The same diagnostic move as the wrapper-overlap diagram's Speed slider. Per Reading 04 (Tier 2 ontological-candidate), speed of perception is itself the compilation rate; per cont 26 §4 Tier 1, it modulates legibility.
L0 Occlusion
0–50% slider		 Probability per frame of triggering a shared substrate-level gap that all observers would experience. At 0% → classical Michotte plus framework Tier 1/Tier 2 effects. Above 0% → injects the Tier 3 speculation from candidates/aux_channel_l0_occlusion.md. Tier 3 speculative — L0 occlusion as auxiliary channel. Exposed as a parameter so users can see what the speculation would look like if it were real. The framework does not endorse this; exposing it as a parameter is the honest way to make a speculation visible without claiming it's happening.

The two new parameters (Attention Sampling, Sensory Bridging) are the heart of the framework-native extension. They turn Michotte's paradigm from an experiment about physical setups into an experiment about observer parameters — which is exactly the move the agnostic framework makes everywhere.

§03What to try — parameter combinations

PRESET 1 · Classical Michotte (baseline)

Mechanical Delay: 50ms · Attention Sampling: 80% · Sensory Bridging: OFF · L0 Occlusion: 0%

Expected: launching. Short delay, high attention, no bridging needed, no shared gaps. This is the original Michotte 1946/63 finding. The verdict panel should read LAUNCHING.

PRESET 2 · Broken Michotte (the gap is too long)

Mechanical Delay: 360ms · Attention Sampling: 80% · Sensory Bridging: OFF · L0 Occlusion: 0%

Expected: disconnected. The delay exceeds the classical visual-only threshold (~100ms). Even with high attention, the observer perceives two separate events. This is the boundary condition that classical Michotte established.

PRESET 3 · Cross-modal rescue (the headline test)

Mechanical Delay: 360ms · Attention Sampling: 80% · Sensory Bridging: ON · L0 Occlusion: 0%

Expected (framework prediction): launching, OR ambiguous. The bridging widens the integration window beyond visual-only threshold. This is the framework-relevant empirical question: does cross-modal integration preserve causality perception at delays where pure visual fails? If YES, the framework's cont 14/16 mapping is empirically grounded at the Michotte paradigm.

PRESET 4 · Attention starvation

Mechanical Delay: 50ms · Attention Sampling: 15% · Sensory Bridging: OFF · L0 Occlusion: 0%

Expected: disconnected or ambiguous. Even classical-Michotte delay fails when attention is too sparse — the observer's samples may miss either contact or B-start. This isolates the perceptual-rate effect (cont 26 §4 Tier 1 canon) from physical timing.

PRESET 5 · L0 occlusion exposed (Tier 3 speculation as visible parameter)

Mechanical Delay: 50ms · Attention Sampling: 80% · Sensory Bridging: OFF · L0 Occlusion: 20%

Expected: variable across runs. Classical Michotte launching when no L0 gap falls in the critical window; disconnected when a shared substrate gap occludes contact or B-start regardless of attention. Run multiple trials — the same parameters produce different outcomes because L0 occlusion is stochastic. This is what the speculation would predict IF the Tier 3 hypothesis were true. The framework does not claim L0 occlusion is happening; the parameter exists to make the speculation legible.

§04What the framework predicts

The simulation embodies framework claims at three tiers. They can be evaluated independently.

Tier 1 (epistemological, canon) — attention sampling modulates causality perception

The cont 26 §4 perceptual-rate-as-sense canon predicts that lowering Attention Sampling should degrade causality perception EVEN AT CLASSICAL-MICHOTTE delays. The simulation embodies this directly: at 10% attention, the observer simply doesn't sample often enough to register both contact and B-start within the integration window. This is empirically testable in classical experimental psychology; the framework's contribution is naming it as a structural feature rather than a perceptual artifact.

Tier 1 (epistemological, candidate-leaning) — sensory bridging extends the launching window

The framework predicts that turning Sensory Bridging ON should preserve causality perception at delays roughly up to ~300ms where pure visual would fail at ~100ms. This is a non-trivial extension of classical Michotte — Michotte's original paradigm is visual-only. Whether the prediction holds empirically in human participants is a real research question; the simulation makes it precise.

Tier 2 (ontological-candidate) — the Speed slider changes compilation rate, not just visualization

Reading 04 maps the diagram's Speed slider against quantum-collapse time-uncertainty physics (Bortolotti et al. 2025). If compilation-rate-as-time holds, then watching the simulation at different Speed settings doesn't just change what you see — it changes the dynamic itself, because time is the compilation rate. The Speed slider exposes this; whether the ontological reading is right depends on the contested physics.

Tier 3 (speculative) — L0 occlusion as auxiliary channel

The L0 Occlusion slider exposes the speculation from candidates/aux_channel_l0_occlusion.md. The speculation predicts that if L0 occlusion is real, paired observers under the same conditions would report SAME-MOMENT gaps (not per-observer gaps). The simulation cannot test this with a single observer; it can only make the speculation's shape visible. Promoting this to Tier 2 requires the bars in /speculations/: physical signature, shared-observer case, or constructive experimental protocol.

§05How to interpret findings

Three reading disciplines, per cont 27 §2 three-tier epistemic procedure:

  • Layer 1 findings stay at Layer 1. If lowering Attention Sampling destroys launching even at short delays, that confirms cont 26 §4 canon at the Michotte paradigm. It does NOT confirm cont 26 §4 at substrate level (that's Layer 2 / Reading 04).
  • Layer 2 findings require external evidence. The Speed slider changing the simulation's behavior is not evidence for compilation-rate-as-time. It's evidence that if the ontological reading is right, you'd see this kind of thing. The contested physics determines whether the reading is right.
  • Layer 3 findings are intuition pumps, not evidence. The L0 Occlusion slider lets you see what L0 occlusion would look like if it existed. That's not the same as evidence it does exist. The bars in /speculations/ specify what would be.

The discipline is cont 27 §2's bright line: don't let Layer 1 evidence get used to claim Layer 3 conclusions. The simulation makes the layers visually distinct precisely because the temptation to laundering is high — three sliders, all producing visible effects, look interchangeable. They are not.
